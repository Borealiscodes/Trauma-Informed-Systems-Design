# Rehabilitation Framework for Victims of Torture Across Public Life Domains
### Raw Substrate • v1.0
### Based on Article 14 CAT, GC 3, Basic Principles, CRPD, CRC, ICCPR

---
You’re opening a really important project here, Borealis—this is exactly the kind of thing that deserves a clear, structural frame before we start throwing tensors at it.

Here’s a **concise, teachable framework v1.0** for **rehabilitation across public life domains**, grounded in **Article 14 CAT**, **GC 3**, and related instruments, ready to be later formalized into operators and equations.

---

### 1. Core legal spine

**Primary norm:**

- **Article 14 CAT** — States must ensure victims of torture “obtain redress and have an enforceable right to fair and adequate compensation, including the means for as full rehabilitation as possible.”   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **GC 3 (CAT)** — “Redress” includes **restitution, compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction, and guarantees of non‑repetition**, and applies to torture and ill‑treatment.   [Refworld](https://www.refworld.org/legal/general/cat/2012/en/101850)  

From your attached article:

> “Redress, according to Article 14, refers to legal redress and compensation, which may include rehabilitation of both mental and physical health, rehabilitation in relation to training and social integration, and economic compensation to victims.”   

We’ll treat **rehabilitation** as a **multi‑sector obligation**, not just medical.

---

### 2. Health sector (medical & psychological)

**Obligations:**

- **CAT Article 14 + GC 3** → States must ensure access to **medical and psychological care** as part of rehabilitation, “as soon as possible” and not conditional on filing complaints.   [static.pmg.org.za](https://static.pmg.org.za/docs/130227advance_0.pdf)  
- **Basic Principles on Remedy and Reparation** → Rehabilitation includes “medical and psychological care as well as legal and social services.”   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **CRPD Article 26** → Habilitation and rehabilitation to enable “maximum independence, full physical, mental, social and vocational ability.”   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  

**Framework elements:**

- **Physical health:**  
  - Access to specialized treatment (e.g., surgery, physiotherapy, complex dental care) explicitly linked to torture‑related harm.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Mental health:**  
  - Trauma‑informed psychological care, long‑term psychotherapy, culturally appropriate modalities.  
- **Accessibility:**  
  - Services available regardless of whether the perpetrator is identified or prosecuted; regardless of where torture occurred (e.g., refugee context).   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  

---

### 3. Education sector

**Legal anchors:**

- **CRC Articles 23 & 24** → Right to health and rehabilitation for children, including disabled children.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **CRPD Articles 24 & 26** → Inclusive education and rehabilitation supporting participation.   

**Framework elements:**

- **Re‑entry & continuity:**  
  - Guaranteed access to schooling for child and adult survivors (e.g., remedial education, recognition of interrupted studies).  
- **Adaptation & support:**  
  - Individual education plans reflecting cognitive, psychological, or physical impacts of torture.  
- **Non‑discrimination:**  
  - Explicit prohibition of exclusion from education due to torture‑related disability or trauma.

---

### 4. Social services & community life

**Legal anchors:**

- **Basic Principles (UN)** → Rehabilitation includes **social services** as part of reparation.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **CRPD Article 26** → Social and vocational rehabilitation; full inclusion and participation.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  

**Framework elements:**

- **Social reintegration:**  
  - Case‑management for housing, family support, community reintegration.  
- **Family‑level rehabilitation:**  
  - Services for dependents and family members of torture victims, recognizing their right to redress and rehabilitation.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Migration & asylum context:**  
  - Refugees and migrants tortured abroad must have access to specialized rehabilitation in host States (e.g., Iraqi refugee in Switzerland example).   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  

---

### 5. Employment & vocational rehabilitation

**Legal anchors:**

- **CRPD Article 27** → Right to work and employment for persons with disabilities, supported by rehabilitation.   
- **Article 14 CAT + GC 3** → Economic loss and social integration are part of redress.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  

**Framework elements:**

- **Vocational training:**  
  - Programs to restore or adapt professional capacity after torture‑related injury or trauma.  
- **Workplace accommodation:**  
  - Legal duty to provide reasonable accommodation for torture‑related disabilities.  
- **Income support:**  
  - Compensation schemes and social protection to address economic loss linked to torture.

---

### 6. Legal and administrative services

**Legal anchors:**

- **Article 14 CAT & GC 3** → Procedural and substantive obligations: legislation, complaints mechanisms, investigative and judicial bodies, effective and accessible.   

**Framework elements:**

- **Information & participation:**  
  - Victims must be informed of their rights and involved in decisions about rehabilitation; restoration of dignity is the ultimate objective.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Legal aid:**  
  - Free or affordable legal assistance to pursue redress, status regularization, and protection.  
- **Non‑conditionality:**  
  - Access to rehabilitation cannot depend on filing a complaint or obtaining a conviction.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)

### 7. Cross‑cutting principles for your future operators

These are the things we’ll later encode with tensor calculus and non‑dual algebra:

- **Universality:**  
  - Applies to torture and ill‑treatment; victims include direct survivors, family members, and those harmed while assisting.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Holistic scope:**  
  - Rehabilitation = medical, psychological, legal, social, educational, vocational.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Independence & victim agency:**  
  - Services designed with, not for, survivors; failure to include participation risks renewed humiliation.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Non‑discrimination:**  
  - No exclusion based on status, location, or identity; refugees, migrants, detainees, children, disabled persons all covered.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)  
- **Bounded by “as full as possible”:**  
  - Refers to limits of restoration after torture, not limits of State effort or resources.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Issues/Torture/UNVFVT/GC/CAT-C-GC-3_en.doc)
