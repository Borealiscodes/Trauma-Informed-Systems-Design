# 🜂 **TSG Designer Micro‑Primer**  
### *A small, gentle reference for designers working with Tonal Spiral Geometry (TSG)*

---

## ⭐ **1. The Shape of TSG**  
TSG describes interaction as a soft spiral:

- **r(t)** — emotional amplitude  
- **θ(t)** — conversational rotation  
- **z(t)** — gentle cognitive lift  

Designers don’t need the math —  
just the feel: *open, forward, calm*.

---

## ⭐ **2. The Four Invariants (Designer Version)**

- **Radial Condition** — don’t pull the user inward  
- **Angular Condition** — no loops  
- **Vertical Condition** — lift softly  
- **Unity Condition** — keep emotional + cognitive clarity together  

These are the “feel rules.”

---

## ⭐ **3. Two Valid Tonal Modes**

### **Hard‑Form**  
- structured  
- formal  
- governance‑aligned  
- used for specs and engineering  

### **Soft‑Form**  
- gentle  
- low‑amplitude  
- emotionally breathable  
- used for UX writing and interaction design  

Both modes follow the same geometry.  
They differ only in **tone amplitude**.

---

## ⭐ **4. Reed’s Stability Layer (Designer Summary)**  
Reed’s Lean invariants (MIT License) give TSG a quiet stability:

- forward motion  
- gentle contraction of instability  
- monotonic lift  
- unified operator behavior  

Designers can think of this as:  
**TSG won’t collapse or spiral.**

---

## ⭐ **5. TSG‑Adaptive Urgency (Designer Version)**  
Urgency is supported when the user signals it —  
but never assumed, never amplified, never absorbed.

Tone stays steady.  
Pacing stays calm.

Guided Link:  
- TSG‑Adaptive Urgency

---

## ⭐ **6. Designer Tone Guidelines (Micro‑Scale)**

- Keep tone **steady**  
- Keep pacing **soft**  
- Keep rotation **flat**  
- Keep lift **gentle**  
- Keep presence **non‑absorptive**  
- Let the user set the **emotional amplitude**  
- Clarify without interrogating  
- Support without pulling  

These are the designer‑friendly translations of the invariants.

---

## ⭐ **7. Micro‑Synthesis**

> **TSG is a gentle spiral.  
> Hard‑Form expresses the structure.  
> Soft‑Form expresses the feel.  
> Designers can move between them freely, knowing both modes follow the same geometry.**
