# **3. Domain Clusters (80‑Dimensional Ecology)**

The sandbox organizes the political ecology into **clusters**, each representing a domain with its own dynamics, risks, and ontological structures.

---

## **Cluster A — AI Regulation Tension**

- **Pro‑industry PACs** push “innovation,” “leadership,” and “common‑sense regulation.”  
- **AI safety PACs** push guardrails, transparency, and existential risk mitigation.  
- “Anti‑regulation” is **poorly defined**, often meaning “no constraints on capital.”

**Key tension:**  
Regulation is framed as a threat to national competitiveness rather than democratic safety.

---

## **Cluster B — Dark Money & PAC Ecosystems**

- Super PACs funnel **tens or hundreds of millions** into primaries.  
- Influence is purchased in **non‑competitive districts** to “pre‑align” future legislators.  
- Billionaire donors create **asymmetric political gravity wells**.

**Key risk:**  
Democratic representation becomes structurally distorted.

---

## **Cluster C — Disinformation & Influence Operations**

- Meme sockpuppets  
- China fear narratives  
- “Doomer playbook” rhetoric  
- Simplified binaries (“doomers vs billionaires”)  
- Narrative flooding

**Key risk:**  
Epistemic collapse — public discourse becomes unmoored from reality.

---

## **Cluster D — Global North Bias**

- U.S.‑centric framing  
- China invoked as a fear lever  
- Global South impacts ignored  
- AI governance treated as a geopolitical race

**Key risk:**  
Ontological narrowing — only two futures are imagined: “America wins” or “China wins.”

---

## **Cluster E — Titanic Ambitions Without Ethics**

- PACs aim to “shape America’s future.”  
- Values are thinly specified.  
- “Leadership” is defined as **technological dominance**, not ethical responsibility.

**Key risk:**  
Ontological nightmare arcs — futures shaped by power, not care.
