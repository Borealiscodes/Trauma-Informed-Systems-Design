# **1. Mission Overview and Timeline**

Europa Clipper is NASA’s flagship ocean‑world mission designed to evaluate Europa’s habitability. Its architecture uses repeated close flybys rather than Europa orbit insertion due to Jupiter’s intense radiation environment.

**Key timeline:**

- **Launch:** October 14, 2024  
- **Arrival at Jupiter:** April 2030  
- **Mission mode:** Jupiter orbit  
- **Europa flybys:** ~49 passes  
- **Closest approach:** ~25 km  
- **Science duration:** Several years  

Each flyby provides a multi‑instrument snapshot of Europa’s:

- ice shell  
- subsurface ocean  
- surface chemistry  
- thermal environment  
- plasma interactions  
- gravitational structure  

This repeated sampling enables coherent manifold construction across the mission.

# **2. Europa Clipper Instruments and Data Types**

Europa Clipper carries nine major instruments plus gravity science. Each instrument contributes a distinct manifold of data.

### **2.1 REASON (Radar)**  
- Ice thickness  
- Subsurface layering  
- Brine pockets  
- Ice roughness  

### **2.2 EIS (Imaging)**  
- High‑resolution surface maps  
- Fracture morphology  
- Chaos terrain characterization  
- Temporal change detection  

### **2.3 Magnetometer (ECM)**  
- Induced magnetic fields  
- Ocean conductivity  
- Salinity constraints  
- Ocean depth modeling  

### **2.4 Gravity Science**  
- Gravitational harmonics  
- Internal density structure  
- Ice shell thickness  

### **2.5 Spectrometers (MAss, MISE, UVS)**  
- Surface composition  
- Organics  
- Salts, oxidants, sulfur compounds  
- Thermal anomalies  

### **2.6 Plasma & Particle Instruments (PIMS, SUDA)**  
- Charged particle flux  
- Neutral gas composition  
- Plume sampling  
- Exosphere dynamics  

These datasets form the empirical foundation for NDH manifold construction.
