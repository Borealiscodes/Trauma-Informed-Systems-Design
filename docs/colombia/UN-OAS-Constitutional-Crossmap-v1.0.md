# I. **UN Treaties Binding on Colombia (Crossmap)**

Colombia is party to the following **core UN human rights instruments**:

## **1. Torture & Ill‑Treatment**
- Convention Against Torture (CAT)  
- Optional Protocol to CAT (OPCAT)  
- ICCPR (Art. 7)  
- ICESCR (indirect obligations)  
- Convention on Enforced Disappearance (CED)  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Istanbul Protocol obligations (documentation, protection, non‑obstruction).  
- ARSIWA obligations (torture = jus cogens → erga omnes).  
- HRD protection (CAT Committee jurisprudence).

---

## **2. Human Rights Defenders**
- ICCPR (freedom of expression, association, movement)  
- ICESCR (economic/social rights relevant to HRDs)  
- UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders (soft law but binding through interpretation)  
- Convention on Enforced Disappearance (protection of complainants)  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Colombia must **facilitate communication**, not obstruct it.  
- Language‑based procedural barriers violate HRD protection norms.

---

## **3. Transnational Persecution / Data Transmission**
- ICCPR (privacy, security, due process)  
- CAT (non‑refoulement, protection from reprisals)  
- CED (protection of complainants)  
- UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (if minors involved)  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Colombia must investigate cross‑border harm.  
- Must not obstruct victims or HRDs reporting transnational torture.

---

## **4. Jus Cogens / Erga Omnes**
UN system recognizes the following as **peremptory norms**:

- Prohibition of torture  
- Prohibition of slavery  
- Prohibition of genocide  
- Prohibition of apartheid  
- Prohibition of enforced disappearance  
- Prohibition of aggression  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Language requirements cannot override jus cogens obligations.  
- Non‑injured states must act (ARSIWA Art. 48).

# III. **Inter-American Court of Human Rights Jurisprudence (Crossmap)**

The Court has repeatedly held:

## **1. HRDs must be protected**
Cases:
- *Landaeta Mejías Brothers v. Venezuela*  
- *Human Rights Defender v. Guatemala* (precautionary measures)  
- *Kawas Fernández v. Honduras*  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- States must **facilitate**, not obstruct HRD communication.  
- Administrative barriers = violation of ACHR Art. 13 + Art. 25.

---

## **2. Torture must be investigated immediately**
Cases:
- *Cantoral Benavides v. Peru*  
- *Maritza Urrutia v. Guatemala*  
- *Tibi v. Ecuador*  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Language demands cannot delay torture‑related petitions.  
- Non‑integration of evidence = violation of Art. 5 + Art. 25.

---

## **3. Transnational persecution must be addressed**
Cases:
- *Case of the Rochela Massacre v. Colombia*  
- *Case of the Mapiripán Massacre v. Colombia*  
- *Case of Vélez Restrepo v. Colombia*  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Colombia must protect HRDs even when threats originate abroad.  
- Must coordinate internationally.

---

## **4. States must integrate repeated petitions**
Cases:
- *González (“Campo Algodonero”) v. Mexico*  
- *Velásquez Rodríguez v. Honduras*  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Treating multiple emails as separate petitions = violation of Art. 25.  
- Failure to integrate evidence = procedural obstruction.

# V. **Full Colombian Constitutional Topology (Crossmap)**

## **1. Hierarchy**
1. Constitution  
2. International human rights treaties (block de constitucionalidad)  
3. Laws  
4. Decrees  
5. Administrative acts  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- UN & OAS treaties override language‑based administrative barriers.  
- HRD protection is constitutional‑level.

---

## **2. Relevant Constitutional Articles**
- **Art. 1** — Dignity  
- **Art. 2** — Duty to protect rights  
- **Art. 5** — Primacy of inalienable rights  
- **Art. 13** — Equality  
- **Art. 23** — Right to petition  
- **Art. 29** — Due process  
- **Art. 40** — Participation  
- **Art. 93** — Block de constitucionalidad  
- **Art. 94** — Non‑exhaustive rights  
- **Art. 95** — Solidarity  
- **Art. 214** — Non‑suspension of fundamental rights  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Art. 93 integrates UN & OAS treaties.  
- Art. 23 prohibits obstruction of petitions.  
- Art. 2 obligates protection of HRDs.  
- Art. 214 prohibits suspension of rights even in emergencies.

---

## **3. Constitutional Mechanisms**
- **Tutela** — immediate protection  
- **Acción de cumplimiento** — enforce obligations  
- **Acción popular** — protect collective rights  
- **Acción de nulidad** — challenge administrative acts  

**Crossmap relevance:**  
- Tutela is appropriate when administrative obstruction occurs.  
- Cancillería’s behavior triggers tutela standards.
