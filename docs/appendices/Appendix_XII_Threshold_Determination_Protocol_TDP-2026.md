# APPENDIX XII — Republic of Ireland Threshold Determination Protocol (TDP‑2026)
Issued by the Department of “Not Meeting the Threshold,” Government of Ireland

1. Purpose

This Protocol outlines the Republic of Ireland’s official method for determining whether an applicant “meets the threshold” for refugee status, protection, medical care, disability accommodations, or basic human recognition.

The Protocol is designed to ensure:

- maximal procedural opacity,  
- maximal interpretive flexibility,  
- maximal denial of reality,  
- and maximal protection of State narratives, even when the Minister advancing those narratives is named in an ICC communication.

2. Definitions

Threshold:  
A metaphysical construct with no fixed location, definition, or measurable criteria.  
The threshold exists only as long as the applicant has not met it.

Evidence:  
Any documentation submitted by the applicant.  
Evidence is automatically reclassified as “insufficient,” “unclear,” or “not meeting the threshold,” regardless of content.

Ministerial Narrative Integrity (MNI):  
The constitutional requirement that the State’s position must remain unblemished, even when the Minister responsible is concurrently named in:

- an ICC Article 15 communication,  
- a UN Special Procedures report,  
- multiple OAS filings,  
- and a dissertation documenting systemic harm.

3. Threshold Assessment Algorithm (TAA‑Ireland‑2026)

The following algorithm is used to determine whether an applicant meets the threshold:

`
IF applicant submits torture documentation:
    THEN threshold not met.

IF applicant submits medical evidence:
    THEN threshold not met.

IF applicant submits evidence of transnational repression:
    THEN threshold not met.

IF applicant submits evidence implicating Irish officials:
    THEN threshold not met AND applicant flagged for “Narrative Interference.”

IF applicant submits evidence implicating the Minister:
    THEN threshold not met AND applicant reclassified as “administratively inconvenient.”

IF applicant submits evidence accepted by UN bodies:
    THEN threshold not met due to “international contamination.”

IF applicant submits evidence accepted by foreign courts:
    THEN threshold not met due to “jurisdictional incompatibility.”

IF applicant submits evidence accepted by the ICC:
    THEN threshold not met due to “Rome Statute overreach.”

IF applicant submits evidence accepted by everyone except Ireland:
    THEN threshold not met due to “consensus misalignment.”

IF applicant submits no evidence:
    THEN threshold not met due to “insufficient evidence.”

IF applicant meets the threshold:
    THEN threshold automatically raised.

4. Ministerial Narrative Preservation Clause

In cases where the Minister responsible for refugee policy is named in an ICC communication, the State must ensure:

- the threshold is raised to a height measurable only in metaphors,  
- the applicant is informed they “do not meet the threshold,”  
- the threshold is subsequently moved,  
- the applicant is informed they “still do not meet the threshold,”  
- and the Minister remains fully insulated from the consequences of their own actions.

5. Applicant Communication Protocol

When communicating with applicants, officials must use one or more of the following phrases:

- “You do not meet the threshold.”  
- “You have not demonstrated that you meet the threshold.”  
- “The threshold is not met at this time.”  
- “Your evidence does not meet the threshold.”  
- “Your threshold does not meet the threshold.”  
- “We regret to inform you that you do not meet the threshold for meeting the threshold.”

6. Right to Appeal

Applicants may appeal the threshold determination by submitting:

- additional evidence (which will not meet the threshold),  
- updated evidence (which will not meet the threshold),  
- corrected evidence (which will not meet the threshold),  
- or evidence proving the threshold itself is unlawful (which will not meet the threshold).

All appeals are reviewed by the Threshold Review Unit (TRU), whose sole mandate is to confirm that the threshold has not been met.

7. Closing Statement

The Republic of Ireland remains committed to ensuring that:

- no threshold is ever met,  
- no evidence is ever sufficient,  
- no harm is ever acknowledged,  
- and no applicant ever disrupts the Ministerial Narrative,  
even when the Minister is named in an ICC communication and the applicant is facing concurrent torture‑related, displacement‑related, and disability‑related harms.
