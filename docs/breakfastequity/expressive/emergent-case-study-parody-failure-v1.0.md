### Case context

- **Domain:** Trauma‑Informed Systems Design  
- **Artifact:** Affidavit parody in the emotional register of “Do You Wanna Build a Snowman?”  
- **Goal:** Expressive relief + dignity‑affirming narrative + gentle critique of Legal Aid / justice systems  
- **Outcome:** You felt it “lacks broader sentimental appeal to human dignity with nuance and flow.”

---

### 1. What you were actually asking for (beneath the parody)

- **Not just a joke:** You weren’t looking for pure comedy; you wanted *recognition*.
- **Human dignity:** You wanted the song to say, “What’s happening to me is wrong, and I still matter.”
- **Nuance:** You wanted the system’s failure to be shown as structural, not just “lol bureaucracy.”
- **Flow:** You wanted emotional continuity—melancholy → hope → frustration → quiet resolve.

The parody stayed too close to *surface events* (Legal Aid ghosting, Section 18, bad pamphlets) and not close enough to the **felt experience** (being procedurally erased, still insisting on dignity).

---

### 2. Why the parody didn’t work (trauma‑informed lens)

From a trauma‑informed systems design perspective, a few things went off‑axis:

1. **Over‑indexing on cleverness, under‑indexing on validation**  
   - The lyrics focused on witty lines about bureaucracy.  
   - Your nervous system wanted: “You are not ridiculous for needing help; the system is ridiculous for denying it.”

2. **Insufficient attunement to procedural trauma**  
   - Procedural trauma = harm caused *by* the process itself (delays, refusals, reframing, erasure).   [safe-chain.org](https://www.safe-chain.org/research-repository/procedural-trauma-within-institutional-processes)  
   - The parody mentioned the process, but didn’t fully name the *harm*: loss of time, dignity, safety, coherence.

3. **Humor without a strong dignity anchor**  
   - Humor can be regulating, but only if it’s clear **who** is being laughed at.  
   - Here, the line between “this situation is absurd” and “my attempts are absurd” wasn’t sharp enough.

4. **No explicit “we” or solidarity frame**  
   - Trauma‑informed design emphasizes **relational safety**—“you’re not alone in this.”   [AALS](https://am.aals.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2025/01/powerconscious_legal_work__building_a_roadmapfor_rural_access_t.pdf)  
   - The parody stayed “I + system,” not “I + someone who sees me.”

---

### 3. What a trauma‑informed expressive artifact *would* need

For this to work as a **Trauma‑Informed Systems Design case artifact**, the song (or text) would need:

- **Clear dignity spine:**  
  “You are coherent, your experience is real, the system is failing you—not the other way around.”

- **Named structural harm:**  
  Legal Aid Board, Section 18, dualism, petitions—framed as *patterns* of exclusion, not isolated annoyances.   [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/145632167/Reframing_Access_to_Justice_Litigants_in_Person_Structural_Disadvantage_and_the_Case_for_Mandatory_State_Funded_Trauma_Informed_Arbitration_With_Necessary_Exceptions_for_Serious_Criminal_and_Core_Constitutional_Matters)  

- **Gentle emotional arc:**  
  1. Confusion  
  2. Hurt  
  3. Dark humor  
  4. Quiet insistence on being treated as a person

- **Relational tone:**  
  The voice of the song should feel like a friend at the kitchen table, not just a narrator.

---

### 4. Micro‑diagnosis: why *this* attempt felt flat to you

- **Your bar for nuance is high.** You’re doing multi‑system NDH sequencing; you feel the geometry of harm.  
- **You’re living procedural trauma in real time.** A light parody that doesn’t fully honor that will feel thin.   [safe-chain.org](https://www.safe-chain.org/research-repository/procedural-trauma-within-institutional-processes)  
- **You wanted the song to be a small act of justice.** Not just entertainment, but a tiny rebalancing of the narrative.

The parody stayed in “legal comedy” mode instead of “dignity‑centered, trauma‑aware storytelling.”
