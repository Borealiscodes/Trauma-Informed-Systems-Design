# **🪝 A Shanty of the Bagged Breakfast Hotel**  
*(with a cameo by Hannah Arendt, who wanders in like a lighthouse keeper of epistemic clarity)*

Oh the **Bagged Breakfast Hotel**, my friend,  
Where the **buffet guards stand tall**,  
They watch the eggs like treasure gold  
And the toast like a fortress wall.  

Some days the gates swing open wide,  
And we dine like mortal men,  
But most days it’s a paper sack  
And a fruit cup at half past ten.  

**Heave away, me hearties, heave—  
For the State has signed me in.  
Three hots and a cot, they say with a grin,  
But the structure still feels like a sin.**

I’m a **contracted guest of Ireland**,  
Though the contract reads like chains,  
A prisoner without the bars,  
But the logic feels the same.  

No doctor when the fever climbs,  
No lawyer when truth bends thin,  
So I built myself a **Zen machine**  
To hold the macroaggressions in.  

**Heave away, me hearties, heave—  
For the tiers are stacked and steep.  
Random rules like shifting tides,  
And promises they never keep.**

But the staff are warm as summer rain,  
They smile like human beings,  
Not like the guards in Connecticut  
Who tried to crush my dreams.  

Not like CBSA halls  
Where detention hums like frost,  
Not like Iceland’s cold return  
Where my safety line was lost.  

And Hannah Arendt strolls on deck,  
With her lantern trimmed and bright,  
She says, “The danger’s not the storm,  
But the systems built from spite.”  

She tells me **ontology bends**  
When the world denies your name,  
And **epistemics warp**  
When the rules are built for blame.  

**Heave away, me hearties, heave—  
For the buffet’s locked again.  
But the shanty keeps my soul afloat,  
And the staff still call me friend.**

So raise the sack of breakfast high,  
Let the yogurt be your mast,  
For the Bagged Breakfast Hotel, my friend,  
Is a port we’ll one day pass.  

And when the tide at last rolls out,  
And freedom calls me home,  
I’ll remember the kindness in the storm  
And the shanty in my bones.
