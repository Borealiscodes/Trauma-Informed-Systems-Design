# **🪝 Shanty of the Half‑Open Doors (Sign‑In Version)**  
### *for the hotel where presence is counted twice and absence is punished once*

Oh the **Half‑Open Doors Hotel**, my friend,  
Where the mornings creak awake,  
And the buffet glows behind its glass  
Like a feast you cannot take.  

The staff they hum a gentle tune,  
They’re kind in all their ways,  
But the rules roll in like winter fog  
And blot out brighter days.  

**Heave and haul, me hearties, haul—  
For the State has penned me tight.  
A guest in name, a ward in truth,  
Held softly in their sight.**

Each dawn I sign my name again,  
A ritual carved in stone,  
For ten days missed — though fed and seen —  
They’ll strike me from my home.  

The ledger knows I ate at dawn,  
It marks me present there,  
But absence from the sign‑in sheet  
Means the State no longer cares.  

**Heave and haul, me hearties, haul—  
For the rules are strange and thin.  
You can vanish while you’re standing there  
If you forget to sign‑in.**

Some days the doors swing open wide,  
And we walk in like we’re free,  
But other days the locks click shut  
And the bagged meal waits for me.  

A yogurt cup, a cold croissant,  
A fruit that’s seen better years,  
And the knowledge that the buffet’s warmth  
Is rationed by unseen gears.  

I’ve slept in cells in Connecticut,  
Where cruelty was the creed,  
I’ve stood in CBSA halls  
Where fear was guaranteed.  

I’ve felt the Icelandic cold return  
Where safety turned to snare,  
So this hotel’s gentle human touch  
Feels almost like repair.  

But Hannah Arendt walks the deck,  
Her coat pulled tight with thought,  
She says, “The harm is not the staff—  
It’s the structure you’re caught.”  

She points toward the sign‑in book,  
Toward the rules that drift like sand,  
Toward the way a human life  
Gets shaped by unseen hands.  

**Heave and haul, me hearties, haul—  
For the buffet’s closed today.  
But the shanty keeps the soul alive  
In its stubborn, salty way.**

So raise your voice, my friend, tonight,  
Let the verses be your chart,  
For the Half‑Open Doors Hotel, my friend,  
Is a harbor for your heart.  

And when the tide at last rolls out,  
And freedom calls your name,  
You’ll carry the shanty in your bones  
Like a lantern’s steady flame.
