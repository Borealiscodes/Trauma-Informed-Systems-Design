---

## **1. Purpose**

The **Stabilization‑Wave Map** models how stabilization:

- emerges locally  
- amplifies  
- propagates across layers  
- synchronizes with temporal curves  
- reinforces holonomy‑level coherence  

It explains:

- why stabilization often arrives in *waves*, not single events  
- how stabilization can be intentionally cultivated  
- how stabilization waves prevent collapse from re‑forming  
- how stabilization interacts with pressure, boundaries, agency, and diagnostics  

This is the structured, rhythmic model of recovery.

---

## **2. Core Concepts**

- **Stabilization Node** — a point where stabilization begins (boundary reinforcement, agency expansion, pressure reduction).  
- **Wavefront** — the leading edge of stabilization as it moves across layers.  
- **Wave Cycle** — the full pattern of stabilization rising, propagating, and settling.  
- **Resonance** — when multiple layers stabilize in synchrony.  
- **Damping** — when stabilization weakens before completing a full wave.  
- **Amplification** — when stabilization strengthens as it propagates.

## **4. Stabilization Wave Phases**

### **Phase 1 — Rising Phase**
A stabilization node forms:

- boundary stabilizes  
- agency expands  
- pressure decreases  
- stabilization vectors increase  

This creates the **initial wavefront**.

---

### **Phase 2 — Propagation Phase**
Stabilization spreads:

- upward into holonomies  
- downward into geometry  
- laterally across sibling layers  

Propagation is strongest when:

- diagnostics confirm stabilization  
- temporal curves shift toward “window”  
- pressure vectors weaken  
- stabilization vectors strengthen  

---

### **Phase 3 — Settling Phase**
Stabilization becomes durable:

- boundaries remain reinforced  
- agency remains open  
- tool‑zone remains viable  
- temporal curves stabilize  
- holonomy layers align  

This is **anchored stability**.

---

## **5. Stabilization Wave Patterns**

### **Pattern A — Local Wave**
Stabilization rises and settles in one layer.  
Useful but limited.

### **Pattern B — Multi‑Layer Wave**
Stabilization propagates across geometry, temporal, and force layers.  
Stronger and more durable.

### **Pattern C — Holonomy Wave**
Stabilization reaches Meta‑Holonomy and Holonomy‑of‑Holonomies.  
System‑wide coherence emerges.

### **Pattern D — Resonant Wave**
Multiple layers stabilize in synchrony.  
This is the strongest form of stabilization.

### **Pattern E — Damped Wave**
Stabilization rises but weakens before propagation completes.  
Recovery remains fragile.
