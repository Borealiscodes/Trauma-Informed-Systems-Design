# TISD-Holonomy-State-Transition-Graph-v1.0.md  
### Trauma-Informed Systems Design  
### Dynamic Transitions Between Holonomy States Under Pressure, Stabilization, Collapse, Recovery, and Interference

---

## 1. Purpose

The **Holonomy‑State Transition Graph** models:

- how holonomy states **evolve over time**  
- how pressure, stabilization, collapse, recovery, waves, and interference **drive transitions**  
- which transitions are **common**, **rare**, **desirable**, or **dangerous**  
- how governance choices can **promote stabilizing transitions** and **discourage collapsing ones**

It is the **dynamic layer** that sits beside:

- the **Holonomy‑Interference Matrix** (static interactions)  
- the **Holonomy‑of‑Holonomies** (structural recursion)  
- the **Recursive‑Stability / Collapse / Recovery / Wave / Interference maps** (behavioral fields).

---

## 2. Holonomy States

The graph includes at least these canonical holonomy states:

- **Coherent** — stabilization‑dominant, anchored stability, resonant waves.  
- **Stabilizing** — recovery cascades active, stabilization waves rising.  
- **Strained** — mixed pressure/stabilization, oscillatory interference, fragile recovery.  
- **Fragmented** — multiple local collapses, weak stabilization, high interference.  
- **Cascading Collapse** — collapse propagation across layers, containment failing.  
- **Contained Collapse** — collapse localized, containment active, recovery possible.  
- **Oscillatory** — rapid shifts between pressure‑dominant and stabilization‑dominant.  
- **Anchored Stability** — durable stability, low collapse risk, strong containment.

## 3. Holonomy‑State Transition Graph (Conceptual)

```text
States:
  C  = Coherent
  S  = Stabilizing
  St = Strained
  F  = Fragmented
  CC = Cascading Collapse
  CT = Contained Collapse
  O  = Oscillatory
  AS = Anchored Stability

Key Transitions (simplified):

  C  <--> AS        (maintenance of stability)
  C  --> S          (minor strain, recovery still active)
  S  --> C          (successful stabilization)
  S  --> St         (stabilization damped, pressure rising)
  St --> F          (strain unresolved, collapse spreading)
  F  --> CC         (collapse becomes cascading)
  CC --> CT         (containment successfully applied)
  CT --> S          (recovery cascades begin)
  St --> O          (oscillatory interference dominates)
  O  --> S or F     (either stabilization gains or collapse spreads)
  AS --> St         (new pressure waves challenge stability)
```

Each arrow represents:

- **driver(s):** pressure waves, stabilization waves, collapse events, containment actions, recovery cascades, interference regimes  
- **risk level:** low, medium, high, critical  
- **governance posture:** maintain, intervene, escalate, retreat.

---

## 4. Transition Drivers

- **Pressure‑Dominant Waves:** push states toward **Strained → Fragmented → Cascading Collapse**.  
- **Stabilization Waves (Resonant/Cascading):** push states toward **Stabilizing → Coherent → Anchored Stability**.  
- **Collapse‑Propagation:** accelerates transitions into **Cascading Collapse**.  
- **Collapse‑Containment:** enables transitions from **Cascading Collapse → Contained Collapse → Stabilizing**.  
- **Recovery‑Cascades:** drive **Contained Collapse / Strained → Stabilizing → Coherent**.  
- **Interference (Mixed/Oscillatory):** keeps systems in **Strained / Oscillatory**, blocking clean transitions.  

---

## 5. Governance Reading

The Holonomy‑State Transition Graph is used to:

- **identify current state** (e.g., “Strained with Oscillatory interference”)  
- **anticipate likely next states** (e.g., “Fragmented or Stabilizing, depending on stabilization wave strength”)  
- **choose interventions** that bias transitions toward stabilizing paths:  
  - amplify stabilization waves  
  - strengthen containment  
  - reduce pressure exposure  
  - enforce diagnostic constraints  
- **avoid governance moves** that push the graph toward **Cascading Collapse** or **Oscillatory strain**.
