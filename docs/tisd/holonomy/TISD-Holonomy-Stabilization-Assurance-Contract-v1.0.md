# TISD-Holonomy-Stabilization-Assurance-Contract-v1.0.md  
### Trauma-Informed Systems Design  
### Guarantees Required for Declaring Coherent or Anchored Stability

---

## 1. Purpose

The **Stabilization Assurance Contract** defines the *non‑negotiable guarantees* that must be satisfied before a system may enter or remain in:

- **Coherent (C)**  
- **Anchored Stability (AS)**  

It prevents:

- premature stability declarations  
- symbolic or policy‑only stability  
- stability claims made while collapse or fragmentation is still active  
- stability labels that hide pressure, collapse, or diagnostic suppression.

This contract is the **final gatekeeper** for holonomy stability.

---

## 2. Assurance Domains

The contract covers five domains:

- **Diagnostic Integrity**  
- **Containment Readiness**  
- **Stabilization Resource Floor**  
- **Collapse‑Signal Reduction**  
- **Holonomy‑State Honesty**

Each domain contains explicit guarantees that must be met.

---

## 3. Stabilization Guarantees

### 3.1 Diagnostic Integrity Guarantee

Before entering C or AS:

- All diagnostics relevant to collapse, pressure, fragmentation, and oscillation must be **visible, unsuppressed, and current**.  
- No diagnostic suppression may be active in **St, F, O, CC**, or during transition into C/AS.  
- Diagnostic transparency must be **continuous**, not momentary.

**If diagnostics are suppressed, stability cannot be declared.**

---

### 3.2 Containment Readiness Guarantee

To enter C or AS:

- Containment capacity must be **available, functional, and non‑collapsed**.  
- The system must be able to respond to new collapse events without entering CC.  
- Containment protocols must be **armed**, not theoretical.

**If containment cannot respond to new pressure or collapse, stability cannot be declared.**

---

### 3.3 Stabilization Resource Floor Guarantee

Stabilization requires:

- sufficient support  
- pacing  
- boundaries  
- tool‑zone viability  
- non‑collapsed agency geometry

The stabilization floor must be **maintained continuously**.

**If stabilization resources fall below the floor, the system must downgrade to St or F.**

---

### 3.4 Collapse‑Signal Reduction Guarantee

Before entering C or AS:

- Collapse indicators must show **real reduction**, not rhetorical reframing.  
- Fragmentation signals must be **decreasing**, not merely ignored.  
- Oscillatory interference must be **resolved**, not masked.

**If collapse signals remain high, stability cannot be declared.**

---

### 3.5 Holonomy‑State Honesty Guarantee

The system must:

- classify itself conservatively  
- avoid optimistic mislabeling  
- avoid skipping stabilizing intermediates (e.g., F → C)  
- avoid “paper stability” (policy language without behavior)

**If honesty cannot be guaranteed, stability cannot be declared.**

---

## 4. Forbidden Stability Declarations

The contract forbids declaring C or AS when:

- diagnostics are suppressed  
- containment is nonfunctional  
- stabilization resources are insufficient  
- collapse signals remain high  
- oscillatory interference dominates  
- the system is in St, F, O, or CC  
- transitions skip required stabilizing states (e.g., CT → C)

These are **invalid stability claims**.

---

## 5. Required Evidence for Stability

To enter C or AS, the system must provide:

- **diagnostic evidence** of reduced pressure/collapse  
- **containment readiness evidence**  
- **stabilization resource evidence**  
- **collapse‑signal reduction evidence**  
- **transition trace evidence** showing a legitimate path (e.g., CC → CT → S → C)

This evidence must be **documented**, not implied.

---

## 6. Stability Revocation Conditions

Stability must be revoked (C/AS → St/F) if:

- diagnostics become suppressed  
- containment capacity collapses  
- stabilization floor drops  
- collapse signals rise  
- oscillatory interference reappears  
- governance violates safety invariants

Revocation is **mandatory**, not optional.

---

## 7. Identity Statement

> **The TISD Holonomy Stabilization Assurance Contract defines the guarantees  
> required before a system may legitimately claim Coherent or Anchored Stability.  
> It enforces diagnostic integrity, containment readiness, stabilization floors,  
> collapse‑signal reduction, and honest state classification, preventing symbolic  
> or premature stability declarations and ensuring stability is earned,  
> demonstrated, and continuously maintained.**
