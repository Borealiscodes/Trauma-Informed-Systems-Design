### Trauma-Informed Systems Design  
### Recovery, Reinforcement, and Expansion Across Geometry, Temporal, Force, Interaction, and Holonomy Layers

---

## 1. Purpose

The **Recovery‑Cascade Map** models how:

- boundary reinforcement  
- agency re‑opening  
- tool‑zone re‑viability  
- pressure reduction  
- stabilization increase  

can **cascade**:

- upward into higher‑order holonomies,  
- downward into local geometry and action fields, and  
- laterally across sibling layers (pressure, stabilization, diagnostics).

It explains how small recovery events can become:

- local repair only, or  
- multi‑layer, system‑wide stabilization and expansion.

---

## 2. Core Concepts

- **Recovery Node:** A point where collapse begins to reverse (boundary re‑stabilizes, agency re‑opens, tool‑zone becomes viable).  
- **Cascade Path:** The route recovery takes through the holonomy stack (upward, downward, lateral).  
- **Stabilization Wave:** A sequence where multiple layers move from strain/collapse into stability.  
- **Anchored Recovery:** Recovery that becomes durable rather than temporary.

## 4. Recovery Cascade Paths

### 4.1 Upward Recovery Path

- Boundary reinforcement or agency reopening  
- → Tool‑zone becomes viable again  
- → Interaction holonomies shift toward “stabilization‑dominant / window”  
- → Meta‑Holonomy moves toward “stabilizing” or “coherent”  
- → Holonomy‑of‑Holonomies enters “coherent” or “stabilizing” mode.

### 4.2 Downward Recovery Path

- Meta‑level recognition of non‑viability and binding safety decisions  
- → Tool‑zone constraints that reduce overload  
- → Boundary reinforcement and reduced exposure  
- → Agency begins to expand again  
- → Local geometry moves from “strained” to “recovering”.

### 4.3 Lateral Recovery Path

- Stabilization vectors increase (support, resources, clarity)  
- → Pressure impact buffered or reduced  
- → Temporal curves shift toward “stabilization‑dominant” or “window”  
- → Diagnostics show fewer breaches and spikes  
- → Sibling layers (boundary, agency, tool‑zone) move into recovery.

---

## 5. Recovery Cascade Modes

| Mode | Description | Upward Effect | Downward Effect | Lateral Effect |
|------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Local Recovery | Recovery stays local | Minimal meta impact | Local repair | Limited cross‑layer |
| Stabilizing | Recovery in multiple local nodes | Meta stabilizing | Local reinforcement | Moderate cross‑layer |
| Cascading Recovery | Recovery spreading across layers | Meta coherence increasing | Widespread repair | Strong cross‑layer stabilization |
| Anchored Stability | Recovery becomes durable | Meta coherence sustained | Local stability maintained | Ongoing cross‑layer resilience |
| Fragile Recovery | Recovery present but unstable | Meta still strained | Local relapse risk | Recovery easily reversed |
