## 1. Purpose

The **Boundary‑Interaction Map** models how:

- **Safety Boundaries**  
- **Agency Fields**  
- **Pressure Vectors**  
- **Stabilization Vectors**

interact as a **dynamic system**, not static components.

This map explains *how safety changes*, *how agency expands or contracts*, and *how pressure and stabilization forces collide* to shape what actions are viable under duress.

---

## 2. The Four Geometry Components

Each component begins with a Guided Link so you can explore it deeper.

- **Safety Boundary** — outer limit of survivable action.  
- **Agency Field** — region of viable action.  
- **Pressure Vectors** — forces that compress safety and agency.  
- **Stabilization Vectors** — forces that expand safety and agency.

## 4. Interaction Modes

### **Mode 1 — Pressure‑Dominant**
Pressure vectors exceed stabilization vectors.

Effects:

- Safety boundary contracts  
- Agency field shrinks  
- Tool zone becomes narrow or non‑viable  
- Action zones collapse into “conditional” or “prohibited”  

### **Mode 2 — Stabilization‑Dominant**
Stabilization vectors exceed pressure vectors.

Effects:

- Safety boundary expands  
- Agency field opens  
- Tool zone becomes viable  
- Action zones shift toward “safe”  

### **Mode 3 — Oscillation Mode**
Pressure and stabilization alternate rapidly.

Effects:

- Boundary instability  
- Agency volatility  
- Tool zone intermittently viable  
- Requires careful pacing and boundary clarity  

### **Mode 4 — Collapse Mode**
Pressure overwhelms stabilization entirely.

Effects:

- Boundary breach  
- Agency collapse  
- Tool zone disappears  
- Only stabilization vectors can restore viability

## 6. Temporal Dynamics

Boundaries and agency are **time‑dependent**.

Pressure and stabilization vectors change:

- hourly  
- daily  
- situationally  
- based on external events  
- based on internal state  

This map assumes **non‑linear temporal behavior**, not static geometry.
