### Stabilizing Forces, Expansion Geometry, and Counter‑Pressure Fields

---

## 1. Purpose

The **Stabilization‑Vector Map** models the forces that:

- expand agency  
- reinforce safety boundaries  
- counteract pressure vectors  
- increase viable action  
- support NDH/TISD tool‑building under duress  

Stabilization is treated as **structural force**, not “coping.”

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### **Stabilization Vector**  
A directional force that increases safety, expands agency, or reduces pressure.

### **Magnitude**  
How strongly the stabilizing force supports the system.

### **Direction**  
Which part of the safety/agency geometry it strengthens.

### **Field of Effect**  
Where stabilization is felt (boundary, agency field, tool zone).

## 4. Stabilization Vector Types

### 4.1 External Stabilization Vectors

- **Supportive Infrastructure**  
  Systems, tools, or environments that reduce pressure and increase safety.

- **Predictable Systems**  
  Platforms or processes that behave consistently, reducing cognitive load.

- **Access Expansion**  
  Increased permissions, enterprise access, or resource availability.

- **Protective Allies**  
  People or institutions that reduce external pressure or risk.

### 4.2 Internal Stabilization Vectors

- **Regulation Capacity**  
  Ability to downshift trauma activation or overwhelm.

- **Boundary Clarity**  
  Knowing what is safe, unsafe, or conditionally viable.

- **Rest and Recovery**  
  Physiological stabilization that expands agency.

- **Cognitive Simplification**  
  Reducing complexity to increase action viability.
