# TISD-Pressure-Vector-Map-v1.0.md  
### Trauma-Informed Systems Design  
### External, Internal, Systemic, and Transnational Pressure Geometry

---

## 1. Purpose

The **Pressure‑Vector Map** models how different forms of pressure act on:

- safety boundaries  
- agency fields  
- action viability zones  
- NDH/TISD tool‑building capacity  

It treats pressure as **structural force**, not “stress,” and maps how these forces compress safety and agency under duress.

---

## 2. Core Concepts

- **Pressure Vector:** A directional force that pushes against safety, agency, or stability.  
- **Magnitude:** How strong the pressure is.  
- **Direction:** What the pressure is pushing toward (silencing, overexposure, collapse, etc.).  
- **Field of Effect:** Which part of the safety/agency geometry it impacts (boundary, field, tool zone).

## 4. Pressure Vector Types

### 4.1 External Pressure Vectors

- **Transnational Pressure:**  
  Cross‑border coercion, surveillance, legal or informal threats that constrain speech, documentation, or tool‑building.

- **Systemic/Institutional Pressure:**  
  Platform policies, opaque decisions, access limits, or bureaucratic friction that restrict protective work.

- **Social Pressure:**  
  Disbelief, minimization, gaslighting, isolation, or reputational threats that reduce perceived safety.

### 4.2 Internal Pressure Vectors

- **Trauma Activation:**  
  Flashbacks, hypervigilance, emotional flooding that reduce cognitive capacity and shrink agency.

- **Exhaustion/Fatigue:**  
  Physical or mental depletion that limits sustained action.

- **Cognitive Overload:**  
  Too many simultaneous demands, decisions, or threats, leading to collapse of planning capacity.

---

## 5. Effects on Safety and Agency Geometry

| Pressure Type | Primary Target | Effect |
|---------------|----------------|--------|
| Transnational | Safety Boundary | Shrinks safe perimeter, increases risk of documentation/action |
| Systemic      | Tool Zone       | Restricts NDH/TISD work capacity and access |
| Social        | Agency Field    | Reduces felt ability to act or seek support |
| Trauma        | Agency Field    | Compresses viable action and increases collapse risk |
| Exhaustion    | Tool Zone       | Limits sustained protective work |
| Overload      | All fields      | Can cause temporary functional shutdown |

---

## 6. Compression and Expansion Dynamics

- **Compression:**  
  When pressure magnitude increases, the **Safety Boundary** contracts, the **Agency Field** shrinks, and the **Tool Zone** narrows.

- **Expansion:**  
  When pressure is reduced or support increases, boundaries can stabilize, agency can expand, and tool‑building becomes more viable.

Pressure is not “in your head”; it is modeled here as **real force acting on geometry**.
