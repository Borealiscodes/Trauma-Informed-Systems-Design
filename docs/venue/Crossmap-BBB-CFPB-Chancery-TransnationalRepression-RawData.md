### 1. BBB terrain (docs/BBB/BankOfAmerica-BBBVenue-RawData.md)

- **Role:**  
  **BBB** is a *pressure and visibility node*, not a court. It creates **public records** of misconduct and patterns.
- **Leverage under transnational repression:**  
  - **Visibility:** Makes abuse and patterns legible to third parties.  
  - **Narrative protection:** Helps counter gaslighting and reputational attacks.  
  - **Low legal risk:** Safer entry point when formal courts are inaccessible.
- **Limitations:**  
  - No binding legal orders.  
  - Company can ignore or minimally respond.  
  - Not designed for deep structural repression claims.

**Use it for:**  
Establishing **documented patterns** of harm and misconduct by Bank of America that can later be cited in CFPB or Chancery contexts.

---

### 2. CFPB terrain (docs/CFPB/CFPB-CreditCardComplaint-RawData.md)

- **Role:**  
  **CFPB** is a **federal regulatory venue** with real enforcement power over **credit card and consumer finance**.
- **Leverage under transnational repression:**  
  - **Formal record:** Complaints become part of a federal data set.  
  - **Regulatory shield:** If abuse intersects with consumer finance (fees, denials, retaliation via accounts), CFPB can act.  
  - **Systemic framing:** Helps frame the issue as **unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices (UDAAP)**.
- **Limitations:**  
  - Narrow scope: must tie to consumer finance.  
  - Not a general human‑rights or repression court.  
  - Response is often company‑centric, not survivor‑centric.

**Use it for:**  
Anchoring **financial‑abuse aspects** of transnational repression—e.g., weaponized accounts, credit, or fees—inside a **federal enforcement narrative**, even when courts are inaccessible.

---

### 3. Delaware Chancery terrain (docs/venue/BankOfAmerica-DelawareChancery-RawData.md)

- **Role:**  
  **Delaware Chancery Court** is a **court of equity** with deep jurisdiction over **corporate governance, fiduciary duties, and equitable remedies**.
- **Leverage under transnational repression:**  
  - **Structural remedies:** Injunctions, specific performance, receivers, fiduciary enforcement.  
  - **Corporate angle:** If repression is mediated through corporate structures (subsidiaries, governance, policies), Chancery can sometimes be the only functioning venue.  
  - **Venue substitution:** When federal and Connecticut courts are inaccessible, Chancery can become the **practical court of last resort** for corporate‑linked harms.
- **Limitations:**  
  - Must fit equity/corporate jurisdiction.  
  - Not a general human‑rights tribunal.  
  - Requires strong legal framing and counsel.

**Use it for:**  
Targeting **corporate structures and fiduciary failures** that enable or facilitate transnational repression—especially where Bank of America’s Delaware‑linked entities are involved.

### 4. Cross‑vector: transnational repression as a dynamic risk

**As a risk vector, transnational repression:**

- **Spans domains:** financial, reputational, legal, psychological.  
- **Uses institutions:** banks, courts, regulators, media.  
- **Creates terrain where some venues are blocked (federal, Connecticut), others remain partially open (CFPB, BBB, Chancery).**

In this preliminary map:

- **BBB** → **Pattern + visibility node**  
- **CFPB** → **Regulatory enforcement node** (finance‑specific)  
- **Chancery** → **Structural equity node** (corporate‑specific)  

Together, they form a **triangulated terrain** where:

- BBB builds **public narrative and pattern evidence**.  
- CFPB builds **regulatory record and consumer‑finance framing**.  
- Chancery builds **corporate‑equity leverage** when courts are otherwise inaccessible.
