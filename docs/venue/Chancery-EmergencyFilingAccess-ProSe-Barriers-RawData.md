**Short takeaway:**  
Delaware Chancery **does allow pro se emergency filings**, but the options are **limited**, **not email‑based for civil actions**, and **require either e‑filing through File & ServeXpress or physical delivery**. Emergency email channels exist **only for guardianship matters**, not for civil injunctions. This creates real barriers for a pro se litigant experiencing judicial harassment or unable to use mail.

## ⚖️ What Delaware Chancery *actually* allows for pro se emergency civil filings  
The most authoritative source is the **Self‑Represented Litigant Quick Reference Guide** from the Delaware Courts. It states:

> “Self‑Represented Litigants (or Pro Se) are permitted to electronically file in civil action cases only… using the Court’s e‑filing provider File and ServeXpress.”  
  [Delaware Courts](https://courts.delaware.gov/Forms/Download.aspx?id=129438)

This is the **only** permitted electronic method for pro se civil filings.

### ✔ Allowed  
- **E‑filing via File & ServeXpress** (paid subscription; fees cannot be waived)  
- **Paper filings by mail**  
- **Paper filings by hand‑delivery to any Register in Chancery office**

### ✘ Not allowed  
- **Email submission of civil emergency filings**  
- **Email submission of civil injunctions**  
- **Email submission of new civil actions**  
- **Email submission of anything except guardianship emergencies**

The court is explicit:

> “Please do not use [the guardianship emergency email] for communication to the Court regarding Civil Action cases. This is strictly for Guardianship Emergencies.”  
  [Delaware Courts](https://courts.delaware.gov/chancery/telephone-kc.aspx)

## 📨 Is there *any* emergency email option for civil injunctions?  
**No.**  
The only emergency email channel is:

- **Chancery_Guardianship_Emergency_Filings@delaware.gov**  
**Guardianship ONLY**  
  [Delaware Courts](https://courts.delaware.gov/chancery/telephone-kc.aspx)

All civil emergency filings (including injunctions) must go through:

- **File & ServeXpress** (e‑filing)  
- **Register in Chancery** (paper)

There is **no civil emergency email inbox**.

---

## 💻 E‑filing barriers for pro se litigants  
The court warns pro se litigants:

> “Pro Se persons should review the fees associated with acquiring and using a Self‑Represented Litigant (SRL) account… All costs or fees… cannot be waived, reduced, or refunded.”  
  [Delaware Courts](https://courts.delaware.gov/Forms/Download.aspx?id=129438)

This means:

- You must pay for a File & ServeXpress subscription.  
- You must pay per‑page docketing fees ($2/page).  
- You must manage technical requirements (formatting, linking, service certificates).  
- You must navigate a system designed for attorneys.

For someone experiencing **judicial harassment**, these barriers are significant.

## 📮 Barriers when mail is not an option  
Chancery explicitly allows:

- **Mail filings**  
- **Hand‑delivery filings**  
  [Delaware Courts](https://courts.delaware.gov/Forms/Download.aspx?id=129438)

But if a litigant cannot safely use mail due to:

- stalking  
- interception  
- judicial harassment  
- transnational repression  
- lack of physical access  
- disability  
- homelessness  
- surveillance

Then the only remaining option is **File & ServeXpress**, which requires:

- payment  
- stable internet  
- technical proficiency  
- ability to safely maintain an account  
- ability to upload notarized affidavits (not trivial under harassment conditions)

This creates a **structural access gap**.

## 🧭 Risk‑terrain analysis: Chancery as “equity venue of last resort” under harassment  
Chancery’s equity mandate historically includes stepping in when **other courts fail or are inaccessible**. But the modern procedural infrastructure creates friction:

### Structural barriers
- No emergency civil email channel  
- Mandatory paid e‑filing system  
- No fee waivers  
- No remote notarization support for pro se litigants  
- No alternative digital intake for vulnerable individuals  
- No protective intake mechanism for harassment victims

### Equity contradiction  
Historically, Chancery’s role was:

> “Equity will not suffer a wrong without a remedy.”

But the **procedural gatekeeping** (paid e‑filing + no email + mail dependence) can prevent a pro se litigant from accessing the court **even when the harm is urgent**.

This is especially acute in **transnational repression contexts**, where:

- mail may be unsafe  
- physical travel may be dangerous  
- digital surveillance may be active  
- judicial harassment may block access to other courts  
- Chancery is the only functioning venue

Thus, the **equity mandate and the procedural reality are in tension**.
