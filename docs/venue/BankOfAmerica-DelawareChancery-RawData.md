## 🧭 **What the Delaware Court of Chancery *is***  
The Delaware Court of Chancery is a **constitutional court of equity**, established in **1792**, and is widely recognized as the **preeminent forum for corporate governance disputes in the United States**. It handles cases **without juries**, using judges (a Chancellor + six Vice Chancellors) who apply principles of fairness rather than strict legal rules.   [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Court_of_Chancery)





---

## ⚖️ **Why it’s so powerful**
Delaware is home to **over two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies**, and more than **1.8 million corporations** — more than the number of residents. Because of this, the Chancery Court has become the **central hub for corporate internal affairs litigation**, including:  
- fiduciary duty disputes  
- shareholder actions  
- merger challenges  
- governance conflicts  
- enforcement of corporate documents  
- equitable remedies (injunctions, specific performance)  

Its judges are career corporate litigators, giving the court unmatched expertise.   [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Court_of_Chancery)

## 🧩 **What “equity jurisdiction” means (and why it matters for your situation)**  
The Chancery Court can only hear cases when:  
1. **The plaintiff seeks an equitable remedy** (e.g., injunction, specific performance).  
2. **The claim itself is equitable in nature** (e.g., breach of fiduciary duty).  
3. **A Delaware statute explicitly grants jurisdiction.**  
  [bloomberglaw.com](https://www.bloomberglaw.com/external/document/X4SAR358000000/litigation-overview-subject-matter-jurisdiction-delaware)

This is important because **equity courts step in when legal remedies fail or are unavailable** — which is exactly the scenario you described (“in the absence of US Federal Court and Connecticut State Court to act lawfully”).

Based on the sources and the court’s jurisdiction, here are the lawful reasons someone might end up in Chancery when other courts aren’t acting:

### 1. **Corporate or fiduciary misconduct involving a Delaware‑incorporated entity**  
If Bank of America or a related corporate structure is incorporated in Delaware (many major financial institutions use Delaware subsidiaries), internal governance disputes can fall under Chancery jurisdiction.

### 2. **Equitable relief is required**  
If the situation requires:  
- an injunction  
- a freeze order  
- specific performance  
- appointment of a custodian or receiver  
- enforcement of fiduciary duties  

Chancery is the correct venue.

### 3. **Failure of other courts to provide an adequate remedy**  
Equity courts historically exist *precisely* for situations where legal courts fail or refuse to act.

### 4. **Corporate documents or agreements governed by Delaware law**  
If any contract, charter, trust, or governance document specifies Delaware law, Chancery becomes the proper venue.
