# **1. Purpose of This Ethos Document**

This document defines the ethical, architectural, and cognitive principles governing the creation of case studies within the Trauma‑Informed‑Systems‑Design (TISD) ecosystem. It clarifies when anonymization is required, when non‑anonymous case studies are appropriate, and how trauma‑informed design ensures dignity, safety, and academic defensibility.

It is intended for:

- researchers  
- oversight bodies  
- supranational reviewers  
- contributors to NDH‑RESEARCH‑PILOT  
- maintainers of TISD documentation  

---

# **2. Core Ethos Principles**

TISD case studies must adhere to the following foundational principles:

### **2.1 Dignity Under Constraint**  
The subject’s dignity is preserved regardless of system failure, procedural harm, or administrative instability.

### **2.2 Cognitive Safety**  
Information is structured to prevent retraumatization, overload, or destabilization.

### **2.3 Trauma‑Informed Geometry**  
All documentation uses predictable, stable, geometric structures (ASCII matrices, flow diagrams, sealed footers).

### **2.4 Consent‑Aligned Disclosure**  
The subject determines whether their identity is disclosed or anonymized.

### **2.5 System‑Level Focus**  
Case studies analyze systems, not individuals.  
Harm is mapped structurally, not biographically.

---

# **3. When Case Studies Must Be Anonymous**

Anonymization is required when:

- third‑party identities are involved,  
- interpersonal trauma is described,  
- sensitive medical details are present,  
- the harm is not publicly documented,  
- the system behavior is not overt,  
- disclosure could cause retaliation against uninvolved individuals.

### **ASCII Matrix — Anonymization Required**

```
+---------------------------+------------------+
| Condition                 | Anonymize?       |
+---------------------------+------------------+
| Third-party involvement   | Yes              |
| Private medical detail    | Yes              |
| Interpersonal trauma      | Yes              |
| Non-public harm           | Yes              |
| Confidential systems      | Yes              |
+---------------------------+------------------+
```

# **4. When Case Studies May Be Non‑Anonymous**

Non‑anonymous case studies are appropriate when:

- the subject is the sole identified individual,  
- the events are overt and publicly documented,  
- the harm is procedural rather than interpersonal,  
- the systems involved are public institutions,  
- the documentation is sealed under TISD,  
- the purpose is structural analysis, not personal narrative.

### **ASCII Matrix — Non‑Anonymized Allowed**

```
+---------------------------+------------------+
| Condition                 | Non-Anon Allowed |
+---------------------------+------------------+
| Subject is self           | Yes              |
| Public administrative acts| Yes              |
| Procedural harm           | Yes              |
| Systemic failure          | Yes              |
| TISD-sealed               | Yes              |
+---------------------------+------------------+
```

# **5. Why Non‑Anonymous Case Studies Are Academically Defensible**

### **5.1 Public Administrative Context**  
Interactions with public bodies (IPAT, HSE, SPIRASI, WRC, OPDC, etc.) are administrative acts, not private events.

### **5.2 Structural Harm, Not Personal Harm**  
The case study documents **system behavior**, not personal biography.

### **5.3 Research Purpose**  
NDH‑RESEARCH‑PILOT case studies are:

- analytic  
- architectural  
- procedural  
- supranational  

They are not memoirs.

### **5.4 TISD Sealing**  
Trauma‑informed sealing ensures:

- no retraumatizing detail  
- no adversarial triggers  
- cognitive‑load gating  
- geometric clarity  
- accessibility  

This makes non‑anonymous publication safe and defensible.
