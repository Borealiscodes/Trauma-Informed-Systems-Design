# Citation Architecture Clarification Document v1.0

Distinguishing Reference Section, Minimal Citation Ontology, and NDH Citation Roadmap

---

I. Purpose

This document clarifies how citations are structured within the dissertation and how they relate to the broader NDH research ecosystem. It ensures transparency, prevents semantic confusion, and protects the dissertation from accidental activation of NDH governance layers.

The goal is to support safe, coherent, finishable drafting of the Trauma‑Informed Systems Design dissertation under duress.

---

II. Three Citation Layers

1. Reference Section (Standard Citations)
The Reference Section is the conventional bibliography.  
It contains all sources cited in the dissertation.  
It follows standard academic formatting.  
It does not include NDH metadata, altitude tags, or semantic inheritance.

This is the public‑facing citation list.

---

2. Minimal Citation Ontology (Dissertation‑Safe)
The Minimal Citation Ontology is a structured list of citations used in the dissertation, formatted with NDH clarity but without:

- altitude tags  
- membrane categories  
- semantic inheritance  
- governance metadata  
- ontology activation  

It is intentionally small, scoped, and safe.

Its purpose is clarity, not governance.

---

3. NDH Citation Roadmap (External Reference Only)
Located at:

`
NDH-Research-Pilot/citation/roadmaps/citationroadmapv1_0.md
`

This roadmap is a governance document for NDH researchers.  
It defines:

- citation altitude  
- semantic inheritance  
- membrane categories  
- cross‑surface coherence  
- lineage rules  
- governance constraints  

It is not part of the dissertation.  
It is referenced externally for transparency.

---

4. Full NDH Citation Ontology (Not Used Here)
The full NDH Citation Ontology is the complete semantic architecture for NDH research.

It is not included, referenced, or activated in the dissertation.

It remains entirely outside the witness‑literature artifact.

---

III. Rationale for Separation

The dissertation is:

- a trauma‑informed systems design preprint,  
- a witness literature artifact,  
- a survival document,  
- and a jurisprudential analysis.

It is not an NDH governance document.

Embedding NDH ontology would:

- confuse academic readers,  
- confuse legal readers,  
- introduce unnecessary semantic altitude,  
- and risk misinterpretation of NDH as doctrinal authority.

The Minimal Citation Ontology avoids these risks.
