# ADDENDUM II — The Tent Instruction ⛺️ Paradox (Public‑Facing Version)

How Ireland Can Deny Housing as a Right While Treating Tent Distribution as “Support”

Date: 29 September 2026  
Location: Dublin, Ireland  
Author: Borealis S. Hedling

---

Why This Addendum Exists

Ireland told a torture survivor on 30 July 2025 to “get a tent” because no shelter was available.

This addendum explains the paradox behind that instruction — a contradiction so stark that it needs to be understood by the public, not just lawyers or policy experts.

---

The Tent Instruction Paradox

Ireland holds two positions at the same time:

1. Ireland says housing is not a right.
This means:

- no constitutional right to housing,  
- no guaranteed access to shelter,  
- no legal remedy if you become homeless.

This is why people are routinely turned away from emergency accommodation.

2. Ireland treats giving out tents as “support.”
Outreach teams — funded by the State — hand out tents when no beds exist.

The public is often told this is “help.”

But tents are not housing.  
They are not shelter.  
They are not safe.  
They are not a solution.

This is the paradox:

> Ireland denies housing as a right, but uses tent distribution to pretend the obligation has been met.

---

Why This Is Dangerous

A tent is not protection.  
It is not dignity.  
It is not safety.

For anyone, it is dangerous.  
For a torture survivor, it is absurd and harmful.

Tents expose people to:

- violence,  
- cold,  
- medical deterioration,  
- isolation,  
- retraumatization.

No human‑rights system in Europe considers tents “adequate shelter.”

---

What European and International Law Actually Says

Ireland is bound by multiple human‑rights treaties that require real shelter, not tents:

European Social Charter
- Article 31: right to housing  
- Article 30: protection from poverty  
- Article 11: right to health  
- Article 13: right to social assistance  

The European Committee of Social Rights has said clearly:

> Tents do not meet the definition of adequate housing.

ICESCR (UN Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights)
- Article 11: adequate housing  
- General Comment No. 4: tents are not adequate housing  
- General Comment No. 7: forced homelessness is a human‑rights violation  

UNCAT (UN Convention Against Torture)
- Article 14: rehabilitation  
- GC3: states must prevent retraumatization  

CRPD (Disability Rights Convention)
- Article 11: protection in emergencies  
- Article 13: access to justice  
- Article 16(4): protection from abuse  

UDHR (Universal Declaration of Human Rights)
- Article 3: security  
- Article 5: dignity  
- Article 25: adequate standard of living  

Ireland is violating all of these when it tells a torture survivor to “get a tent.”

---

Why This Matters for the Public

This is not a niche legal issue.  
It is a public‑interest issue.

It shows:

- how homelessness policy has collapsed,  
- how human dignity is being eroded,  
- how vulnerable people are being abandoned,  
- how international obligations are being ignored,  
- and how tents have become a symbol of systemic failure.

Ireland is the only EU country where tents are routinely given out instead of shelter — and where this practice is treated as “support.”

---

What Solidarity Looks Like

Solidarity does not require confrontation.  
It requires visibility.

People can help by:

- acknowledging the absurdity of the tent instruction,  
- sharing this information publicly,  
- supporting human‑rights defenders,  
- asking why tents have become normal,  
- and refusing to accept homelessness as inevitable.

Ireland is better than this.  
Its people are better than this.  
Its institutions must be held to the same standard.

---

Conclusion

The Tent Instruction Paradox reveals a simple truth:

> If a country denies housing as a right, tents become its policy.  
> If a country respects human dignity, tents are never an acceptable answer.

This addendum is public‑facing because the public deserves to know how far the system has drifted from its obligations — and how urgently solidarity is needed.
