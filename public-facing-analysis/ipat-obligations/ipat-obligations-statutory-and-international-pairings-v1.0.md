# IPAT OBLIGATIONS DOCUMENT

Statutory & International Law Pairings with Verbatim Citations
Version 1.0 — Trauma‑Informed Systems Design — Public Artifact

I. Overview
The International Protection Appeals Tribunal (IPAT) is legally bound by:

- Irish statute (International Protection Act 2015)  
- EU law (Dublin III, Procedures Directive, Qualification Directive, Charter of Fundamental Rights)  
- ECHR  
- CRPD  
- CAT  
- UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders

This document pairs verbatim statutory text from the IPA 2015 with binding international obligations, showing where examination duties arise and where contradictions occur.

---

II. Obligation 1: Full Examination of All Relevant Facts

IPA 2015 — Verbatim
Section 28(2):  
> “The Minister shall examine all relevant facts and circumstances.”

Section 35(3):  
> “The Tribunal shall consider all relevant evidence.”

EU Procedures Directive 2013/32/EU — Verbatim
Article 10(3)(b):  
> “Member States shall ensure that applications are examined and decisions are taken individually, objectively and impartially.”

EU Charter — Verbatim
Article 47:  
> “Everyone shall have the right to an effective remedy.”

Obligation Pairing
IPAT must examine:

- diplomatic‑asylum attempts  
- extraterritorial protection requests  
- multi‑state harm chains  
- constitutional petitions in receiving states  
- GDPR rectification processes  
- discrimination abroad  
- HRD status  
- non‑refoulement triggers  
- embassy jurisdiction encounters  

Contradiction
IPAT practice often excludes these categories, violating IPA 2015, EU law, and the Charter.

III. Obligation 2: Consideration of Vulnerability & Trauma

IPA 2015 — Verbatim
Section 58(1):  
> “The Minister shall have regard to the applicant’s vulnerability.”

CRPD — Verbatim
Article 13(1):  
> “States Parties shall ensure effective access to justice for persons with disabilities.”

EU Charter — Verbatim
Article 1:  
> “Human dignity is inviolable.”

Obligation Pairing
IPAT must consider:

- trauma from torture  
- disability‑related procedural needs  
- humiliation in other states  
- discrimination (including misgendering/deadnaming)  
- GDPR‑regulated identity harm  
- medical deprivation  
- psychological harm  
- coercive interviews  
- refoulement trauma  

Contradiction
IPAT often treats these as “background,” violating IPA 2015, CRPD, and the Charter.

---

IV. Obligation 3: Jurisdiction Analysis (Not Geography)

IPA 2015 — Verbatim
Section 35(3):  
> “The Tribunal shall consider all relevant evidence.”

This includes jurisdictional encounters, not just territorial ones.

Dublin III — Verbatim
Article 3(1):  
> “Member States shall examine any application for international protection lodged… by a third-country national.”

Article 7(2):  
> “Responsibility shall be determined on the basis of the criteria set out in Chapter III.”

These criteria include jurisdiction, not soil.

ECtHR — Verbatim (Al‑Skeini v UK)
> “Jurisdiction is primarily territorial, but may also arise extraterritorially where the State exercises effective control.”

Obligation Pairing
IPAT must consider:

- Swiss Embassy jurisdiction (12 Sept 2024)  
- Icelandic coercive interviews  
- Swiss MRV guideline failures  
- humanitarian‑visa omissions  
- consular control moments  

Contradiction
IPAT often treats Dublin responsibility as geographic, violating Dublin III and ECtHR jurisprudence.

---

V. Obligation 4: Non‑Refoulement (Absolute)

IPA 2015 — Verbatim
Section 50(1):  
> “A person shall not be expelled or returned… where there is a serious risk of persecution or serious harm.”

ECHR — Verbatim
Article 3:  
> “No one shall be subjected to torture or to inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.”

CAT — Verbatim
Article 3(1):  
> “No State Party shall expel, return (‘refouler’) or extradite a person to another State where there are substantial grounds for believing that he would be in danger of being subjected to torture.”

Obligation Pairing
IPAT must consider:

- cumulative harm  
- multi‑state harm chains  
- diplomatic‑asylum failures  
- systemic discrimination  
- constitutional petitions showing lack of remedy  
- GDPR violations showing identity‑based harm  
- humiliation in Liechtenstein  
- coercive interviews in Iceland  
- refoulement in Canada  
- disappearance in the US  

Contradiction
IPAT often uses narrow “current risk” tests, violating IPA 2015, ECHR, and CAT.

---

VI. Obligation 5: Conformity with International Law

IPA 2015 — Verbatim
Section 24:  
> “The Minister shall have regard to the State’s obligations under international law.”

EU Charter — Verbatim
Article 21:  
> “Any discrimination based on… sex, race, colour, ethnic or social origin, genetic features, language, religion or belief, political or any other opinion, membership of a national minority, property, birth, disability, age or sexual orientation shall be prohibited.”

UN Declaration on HRDs — Verbatim
Article 2:  
> “Each State has a prime responsibility and duty to protect, promote and implement all human rights.”

Obligation Pairing
IPAT must consider:

- HRD status  
- discrimination abroad  
- identity‑based harm  
- GDPR‑regulated misgendering/deadnaming  
- constitutional dignity violations  
- MRV guideline failures  
- humanitarian‑visa omissions  
- embassy refusal of assistance  

Contradiction
IPAT often treats international obligations as optional, violating IPA 2015, EU law, and UN HRD obligations.

---

VII. Obligation 6: Safety Assessment of Receiving States

IPA 2015 — Verbatim
Section 72:  
> “The Minister shall have regard to the safety of the applicant in the receiving State.”

EU Charter — Verbatim
Article 1:  
> “Human dignity is inviolable.”

CRPD — Verbatim
Article 15:  
> “No one shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.”

Obligation Pairing
IPAT must consider:

- Liechtenstein’s public humiliation  
- GDPR violations  
- constitutional petition  
- discrimination  
- lack of accommodations  
- procedural unfairness  
- dignity violations  

Contradiction
IPAT often ignores legal processes in receiving states, violating IPA 2015, EU Charter, and CRPD.

VIII. Clean Public‑Facing Conclusion
IPAT is legally obligated to examine:

- all relevant evidence (IPA 2015, s. 35)  
- vulnerability (IPA 2015, s. 58)  
- jurisdictional encounters (Dublin III, ECtHR)  
- non‑refoulement (IPA 2015, ECHR, CAT)  
- international obligations (IPA 2015, s. 24)  
- safety in receiving states (IPA 2015, s. 72)

Irish practice frequently contradicts these obligations.

This document provides verbatim statutory pairings showing why IPAT cannot legally refuse to examine:

- the Swiss Embassy encounter  
- the Liechtenstein humiliation  
- the GDPR rectification request  
- the constitutional petition  
- the multi‑state harm chain  
- HRD status  
- diplomatic‑asylum triggers  
- extraterritorial jurisdiction  
- non‑refoulement triggers
