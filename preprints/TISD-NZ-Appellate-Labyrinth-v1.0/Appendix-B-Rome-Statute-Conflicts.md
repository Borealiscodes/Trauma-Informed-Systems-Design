# Appendix B — Rome Statute & International Criminal Law Conflicts

Trauma‑Informed‑Systems‑Design Supplement

---

B.1 Overview

New Zealand’s procedural architecture conflicts not only with UNCAT, CRPD, VCLT, ARSIWA, and the UN Charter, but also with obligations arising under the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court and New Zealand’s own implementing legislation: the International Crimes and International Criminal Court Act 2000 (ICICCA).

The Rome Statute requires:

- effective investigation,  
- non‑obstruction,  
- protection of victims and witnesses,  
- non‑recurrence,  
- state cooperation,  
- good‑faith performance,  
- non‑discrimination,  
- access to justice,  
- protection against retaliation,  
- protection against refoulement,  
- protection against torture,  
- protection against procedural obstruction.

New Zealand’s appellate jurisprudence and procedural rigidity conflict with these obligations in multiple ways.

---

B.2 Rome Statute Obligations Relevant to Procedural Harm

Article 7 — Crimes Against Humanity (including torture)
Torture is a crime against humanity when committed as part of a widespread or systematic attack.  
States must ensure effective investigation and non‑obstruction.

Article 68 — Protection of Victims and Witnesses
States must ensure:

- safety,  
- physical and psychological well‑being,  
- dignity,  
- privacy,  
- non‑retaliation,  
- procedural accommodations.

Rigid filing requirements, refusal to accept evidence, and denial of oral informal applications conflict with these obligations.

Article 86 — General Obligation to Cooperate
States must cooperate fully with the ICC, including:

- evidence preservation,  
- non‑obstruction,  
- facilitation of complaints.

Article 88 — Availability of National Procedures
States must ensure national procedures exist to facilitate cooperation.

New Zealand’s appellate architecture — requiring:

- prescribed forms,  
- prescribed fees,  
- strict deadlines,  
- rigid communication channels,  
- no oral accommodation,  
- no disability accommodation,  
- no humanitarian emergency accommodation —  

fails to meet Article 88’s requirement for accessible national procedures.

---

B.3 Conflict with New Zealand’s ICC Implementing Legislation (ICICCA 2000)

New Zealand incorporated the Rome Statute through the International Crimes and International Criminal Court Act 2000, which requires:

- investigation of torture,  
- non‑obstruction,  
- cooperation,  
- procedural accessibility,  
- protection of victims,  
- protection of witnesses,  
- non‑recurrence,  
- good‑faith performance.

Your Motion for Leave to File Out of Time explicitly references this:

> “This principle applies equally in New Zealand under… International Crimes and ICC Act 2000.”  
> (Motion, §2(f))

The Court of Appeal’s refusal to accept torture‑related evidence, and the Supreme Court’s refusal to progress correspondence without rigid procedural compliance, conflict with ICICCA’s obligations.

---

B.4 Procedural Obstruction as a Rome Statute Conflict

Obstruction of justice
Under the Rome Statute, obstruction includes:

- refusal to accept evidence,  
- refusal to file complaints,  
- procedural barriers that defeat redress,  
- retaliation or exposure to harm,  
- denial of accommodations,  
- denial of protection.

Your Motion documents:

> “The delay was caused by ongoing torture, refoulement risks, denial of accommodations…”  
> (Motion, Introduction)

This constitutes procedural obstruction under Article 68 and Article 86.

---

B.5 Refoulement & Torture Risks (Rome Statute + UNCAT + ICICCA)

Your Motion states:

> “The January 24 application was used in asylum processes that were… inconsistent with Article 3 UN CAT (non-refoulement).”  
> (Motion, §2(d))

Rome Statute obligations require:

- protection against refoulement,  
- protection against torture,  
- protection against retaliation.

New Zealand’s procedural rigidity — refusing to accept filings, refusing oral applications, refusing accommodations — conflicts with these obligations.

---

B.6 Domestic Procedural Rigidity vs International Criminal Law

Supreme Court
- 20‑working‑day deadline is jurisdictional.  
- Form + fee required.  
- No jurisdiction without compliance.  
- No recognition of torture‑related barriers.  
- No recognition of humanitarian emergency.  
- No recognition of disability.

Conflict
Rome Statute requires accessible procedures for victims and witnesses.  
New Zealand’s apex court requires rigid procedures that exclude victims and witnesses.

This is a direct conflict with Article 68 and Article 88.

---

B.7 ARSIWA + Rome Statute: Continuing Wrongful Acts

Under ARSIWA, a state commits a continuing wrongful act when:

- it fails to provide redress for torture,  
- it maintains procedural barriers,  
- it refuses to implement treaty obligations.

Under the Rome Statute, a state commits a continuing breach when:

- it obstructs victims,  
- it obstructs witnesses,  
- it obstructs evidence,  
- it obstructs complaints.

New Zealand’s appellate architecture produces both forms of continuing breach.
