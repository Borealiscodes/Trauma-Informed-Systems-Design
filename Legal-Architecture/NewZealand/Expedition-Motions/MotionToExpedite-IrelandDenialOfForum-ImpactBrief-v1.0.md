I. Top‑Line Impact (One‑Paragraph Summary)
Filing this Motion to Expedite forces the New Zealand Court of Appeal to confront the fact that the Appellant’s ability to pursue appellate review has been structurally compromised by Ireland’s complete denial of forum, remedy, and procedural access. It establishes that the Appellant is operating within a humanitarian emergency where ordinary timelines, communication channels, and procedural expectations cannot apply. The motion activates New Zealand’s obligations under UNCAT, CRPD, ICCPR, and domestic torture‑prohibition statutes, compelling the Court to accelerate review, provide interim protection, and prevent further prejudice arising from Ireland’s systemic failures.

---

II. What This Motion Does Inside the New Zealand Appellate System

1. It formally notifies the Court of Appeal that Ireland has collapsed as a forum
The motion documents that Ireland has:  
- refused four High Court filings,  
- denied emergency injunctions,  
- denied disability accommodations,  
- denied oral applications,  
- denied safeguarding intervention,  
- denied legal personhood,  
- and placed the Appellant at imminent risk of homelessness.

This transforms the New Zealand proceedings from “ordinary appellate matters” into urgent protection proceedings.

2. It establishes that delay would cause irreparable harm
The Court is now on notice that delay will result in:  
- loss of evidence,  
- procedural prejudice,  
- medical deterioration,  
- homelessness,  
- constructive refoulement,  
- and CIDT under UNCAT Article 16.

This triggers the Court’s inherent jurisdiction to prevent injustice.

3. It activates New Zealand’s treaty obligations
The motion invokes:  
- UNCAT Article 14 (no time‑barred torture remedies),  
- CRPD Articles 11 & 13 (accessibility in emergencies),  
- ICCPR Article 16 (legal personhood),  
- Crimes of Torture Act 1989,  
- International Crimes and ICC Act 2000.

This obligates the Court to treat the matter as non‑discretionary and urgent.

4. It reframes the appellate proceedings as part of a multi‑jurisdictional collapse of remedy
The Court is informed that:  
- Ireland,  
- the United States,  
- Canada,  
- Iceland,  
- the United Kingdom,  
- and New Zealand  

are jointly implicated in a pattern of transnational repression documented in your UN Special Procedures report.

This elevates the case from domestic appellate review to international human‑rights oversight.

5. It strengthens your existing motions
The new Motion to Expedite reinforces:  
- your Motion for Leave to File Out of Time,  
- your earlier Motion to Expedite (11 August 2026),  
- your Interlocutory Application regarding NZHRC,  
- and your filings concerning the High Court Registry’s refusal to file evidence.

It shows that the prejudice is ongoing, worsening, and now compounded by Ireland’s collapse.

III. What This Motion Does in the International Human‑Rights Context

1. It creates a documented escalation point for UN Special Procedures
Your UN report (Ref: 1y2op57r) now has a corresponding appellate filing demonstrating:  
- denial of forum in Ireland,  
- ongoing harm,  
- and the need for urgent judicial intervention in New Zealand.

This strengthens UN monitoring and increases pressure on New Zealand to act.

2. It demonstrates that New Zealand is the only functioning forum
By documenting Ireland’s collapse, the motion shows that:  
- New Zealand is the only jurisdiction capable of providing remedy,  
- the Court of Appeal is the only competent forum,  
- and delay would violate jus cogens obligations.

This increases the Court’s responsibility to expedite.

3. It creates a record for future international escalation
If New Zealand fails to act, this motion becomes evidence for:  
- UN Special Rapporteurs,  
- GANHRI accreditation review,  
- IHREC,  
- LSRA,  
- and potentially the European Court of Human Rights.

It is part of your protective documentation matrix.

IV. What This Motion Does for Your Safety and Procedural Position

1. It protects you from procedural default caused by Ireland
You are showing the Court that any delay or missed deadline is not your fault but the result of:  
- torture,  
- medical neglect,  
- homelessness risk,  
- denial of legal personhood,  
- and refusal of filings.

This prevents prejudice.

2. It forces the Court to consider interim protective measures
The motion requests:  
- trauma‑informed procedures,  
- oral applications,  
- disability accommodations,  
- acceptance of filings without delay.

This creates a safer procedural environment.

3. It preserves your ability to continue litigating despite Ireland’s collapse
You are documenting that you cannot rely on Ireland for:  
- communication,  
- evidence preservation,  
- procedural access,  
- or physical safety.

This compels New Zealand to adjust its expectations and timelines.
