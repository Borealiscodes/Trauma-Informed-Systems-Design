# Integrated Analysis — SOAS, IPAT, and Article 14 UN CAT (General Comment No. 3)
A Coherent Pattern of Institutional, Civil‑Society, and Regulatory Failures

I. Introduction

The failures of SOAS University of London and the International Protection Appeals Tribunal (IPAT) in Ireland cannot be understood in isolation. They form part of a coherent, cross‑jurisdictional pattern of institutional exclusion, civil‑society avoidance, and regulatory inaction that directly violates Article 14 of the UN Convention Against Torture (UN CAT) and General Comment No. 3 (GC3).

Both institutions have:

- denied access to justice,  
- denied disability accommodations,  
- ignored torture disclosures,  
- refused trauma‑informed procedures,  
- and treated a torture survivor’s attempts to seek rehabilitation as administrative inconveniences rather than non‑derogable rights.

This pattern demonstrates systemic failure across the UK and Ireland to uphold the obligations outlined in Article 14 UN CAT and GC3.

---

II. Article 14 UN CAT and General Comment No. 3 — The Core Obligations

GC3 makes clear that Article 14 requires States to provide:

- “as full rehabilitation as possible” (GC3 ¶12),  
- holistic services including medical, psychological, legal, and social support (GC3 ¶11),  
- accessible procedures that do not depend on formal complaints (GC3 ¶15),  
- effective bodies capable of receiving and processing claims (GC3 ¶5),  
- non‑retaliation,  
- non‑discrimination,  
- victim participation,  
- and guarantees of non‑repetition.

The attached academic article emphasizes that rehabilitation is not merely medical:

> “Rehabilitation… includes medical and psychological care as well as legal and social services.”  
> (Basic Principles, §21 — quoted in the article)

And that:

> “Giving voice to and respecting the decisions and agency of torture survivors are vital components of a process of recovering life and dignity.”  
> (Sveaass, Gaer & Grossman)

Both SOAS and IPAT have violated these principles.

---

III. SOAS — Failure to Provide Rehabilitation Through Access to Education

1. Denial of Oral Accommodations
SOAS refused to provide trauma‑informed oral application pathways despite repeated disclosures of PTSD, communication impairments, and torture history.

This violates:

- CRPD Article 16(4),  
- CRPD Article 13,  
- Article 14 UN CAT,  
- GC3’s requirement for accessible procedures,  
- and GC3’s requirement for victim participation.

2. Failure to Acknowledge Transnational Repression
SOAS ignored disclosures of:

- torture,  
- kidnapping,  
- refoulement threats,  
- denial of legal personhood,  
- collapse of remedy across jurisdictions.

This violates GC3’s requirement that rehabilitation must be holistic, context‑aware, and responsive to the gravity of violations.

3. Fragmentation of Responsibility
SOAS diffused responsibility across:

- Admissions,  
- Scholarships,  
- SID,  
- Disability Services,  
- FOI,  
- Senior leadership.

GC3 requires effective and accessible bodies. SOAS provided neither.

4. Failure to Provide Guarantees of Non‑Retaliation
Your request for a guarantee that FOI activity would not bias scholarship review was ignored.

This violates Article 13 UN CAT and GC3’s non‑retaliation obligations.

---

IV. IPAT — Failure to Provide Rehabilitation Through Access to Justice

1. Treating the Government of Ireland as a Respondent Instead of Conducting Ex Parte Review
In cases involving torture, GC3 requires protection‑oriented procedures, not adversarial ones.

IPAT’s adversarial posture:

- empowers the State,  
- disadvantages the torture survivor,  
- and violates Article 14’s requirement for effective redress.

2. Legal Aid Exclusion Makes Counsel Impossible
IPAT claims you have an “opportunity to obtain counsel,” but:

- Legal Aid refused assistance,  
- demanded re‑submission under a dead name,  
- ignored torture evidence,  
- ignored disability disclosures.

GC3 requires accessible bodies and non‑discriminatory procedures. IPAT’s framing is a procedural fiction.

3. Denial of Medical Care and SPIRASI Services
Without:

- medical stabilization,  
- trauma‑informed assessment,  
- rehabilitation services,  
- SPIRASI access,

participation in legal proceedings is impossible.

GC3 requires rehabilitation “as soon as possible following assessment by qualified independent medical professionals.”  
Ireland has provided none.

4. Civil Society Avoidance Mirrors SOAS’s Failures
Across Ireland:

- HSE Safeguarding refused to act,  
- NGOs refused involvement,  
- law firms dismissed torture disclosures,  
- universities issued bans or refused complaints,  
- oversight bodies ignored filings.

IPAT’s failures are part of this broader collapse.

---

V. Coherent Pattern of Institutional and Civil‑Society Failure

Across the UK and Ireland, institutions have:

- ignored torture disclosures,  
- denied accommodations,  
- refused oral procedures,  
- labeled legitimate complaints “vexatious,”  
- issued exclusion orders,  
- refused to process filings,  
- denied legal personhood,  
- and avoided responsibility.

This pattern violates:

- Article 14 UN CAT,  
- Article 13 UN CAT,  
- Article 16(4) CRPD,  
- Article 13 CRPD,  
- GC3’s holistic rehabilitation requirements,  
- GC3’s accessibility requirements,  
- GC3’s non‑retaliation requirements,  
- and GC3’s victim‑participation requirements.

---

VI. Conclusion

SOAS and IPAT are not isolated failures.  
They are manifestations of a systemic, cross‑jurisdictional collapse of remedy.

Both institutions have violated:

- Article 14 UN CAT,  
- General Comment No. 3,  
- CRPD Articles 11, 13, and 16(4),  
- and their own domestic obligations.
