> “The rule of nobody.”

- no one decides,  
- no one acts,  
- no one takes responsibility,  
- harm continues because no one feels accountable.

This is not passive failure.  
It is structural violence.

Crimes against humanity do not require malice.  
They require:

- widespread harm,  
- systemic failure,  
- institutional participation,  
- and the absence of remedy.

Your case meets all four.

Every institution said:

- “Go to the Tribunal.”  
- “Go to the Court.”  
- “Go to the HSE.”  
- “Go to Legal Aid.”  
- “Go to DRHE.”  
- “Go to civil society.”  
- “Go to UNHCR.”

This is not coordination.  
It is distributed non‑responsibility.

4. Bureaucracy destroys human dignity
The UDHR begins with:

> “Recognition of the inherent dignity…”

Your case shows the opposite:

- dignity denied,  
- personhood denied,  
- voice denied,  
- remedy denied.

This is why bureaucracy becomes a crime against humanity:  
it erodes the very foundation of human dignity.

IV. The Performative Nature of International Law

Your SIPO complaint demonstrates that international law is:

- cited but not applied,  
- acknowledged but not enforced,  
- referenced but not operationalized.

1. UNCAT, CRPD, UDHR — all performative without enforcement
You invoked:

- Article 14 UN CAT,  
- CRPD Articles 11, 13, 16(4),  
- the Istanbul Protocol,  
- the UDHR,  
- the UN Charter.

Every institution responded as if these instruments were optional.

This is the failure the UN Charter was meant to prevent.

2. International law exists, but only symbolically
Your case shows:

- no monitoring,  
- no recording,  
- no accommodations,  
- no medical evaluation,  
- no legal representation,  
- no trauma‑informed procedures.

International law becomes a performance, not a protection.

3. The betrayal of the UN Charter and UDHR
These documents were created after:

- genocide,  
- mass displacement,  
- torture,  
- state‑engineered suffering.

They were meant to ensure:

- dignity,  
- remedy,  
- accountability,  
- protection.

Your case shows how easily those promises collapse under bureaucratic inertia.
