# **Formal Erga Omnes Analysis (2024–2026 authoritative synthesis)**

## **1. Definition and Core Concept**

The modern definition of **obligations erga omnes** comes from the International Court of Justice’s *Barcelona Traction* judgment (1970), where the Court stated that certain obligations are:

> **“owed to the international community as a whole.”**  
  [Oxford Public International Law](https://opil.ouplaw.com/display/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400?d=/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400&p=emailAyklF7.cHLMDM)

This means:

- They are **not owed to a specific state**,  
- but to **all states collectively**,  
- and **all states have a legal interest** in their protection.

This is the foundational doctrinal point.

---

## **2. What qualifies as an Erga Omnes obligation?**

According to the Max Planck Encyclopedia (2024 update):

- Erga omnes obligations arise from norms protecting **fundamental community interests**.  
- They include obligations relating to:  
  - **prohibitions on genocide**  
  - **prohibitions on slavery**  
  - **prohibitions on racial discrimination / apartheid**  
  - **prohibitions on aggression**  
  - **the right to self‑determination**  
  [Oxford Public International Law](https://opil.ouplaw.com/display/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400?d=/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400&p=emailAyklF7.cHLMDM)

These are obligations where **every state** has standing to invoke responsibility.

---

## **3. Relationship to Jus Cogens (Peremptory Norms)**

The ILC (2025 report) explains the relationship clearly:

- **Jus cogens** = *non‑derogable norms* with hierarchical superiority.  
- **Erga omnes** = *obligations owed to all states* (structure of obligation).  
  [United Nations - Office of Legal Affairs](https://legal.un.org/ilc/reports/2025/english/annex3.pdf)

They often overlap:

- Most jus cogens norms **generate** erga omnes obligations.  
- But they are **not identical** concepts.  
  [sustainablecatalyst.com](https://sustainablecatalyst.com/jus-cogens-erga-omnes-and-peremptory-norms-in-international-law/)

**Key distinction:**

| Concept | Nature | Consequence |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Jus Cogens** | Hierarchical norm | Treaties conflicting with it are void |
| **Erga Omnes** | Structure of obligation | All states may invoke responsibility |

## **4. ICJ’s articulation problems (2025 scholarly consensus)**

Priya Urs (Oxford, 2025) demonstrates that the ICJ’s treatment of erga omnes is:

- **inconsistent**,  
- **unclear**,  
- and **varies across cases**.  
  [Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/articulation-of-obligations-erga-omnes-and-erga-omnes-partes-by-the-international-court-of-justice-coherence-or-confusion/3DF4307A85200A6A6C7C891FC1FE1C7E)

The ICJ has:

- failed to define **criteria** for identifying erga omnes obligations,  
- blurred distinctions between **erga omnes** and **erga omnes partes**,  
- left **standing** and **enforceability** uncertain.

This doctrinal ambiguity is now recognized as a **major enforcement problem**.

---

## **5. ILC’s current position (2025–2026)**

The ILC’s 2025 proposal on erga omnes obligations states:

- Erga omnes obligations reflect **“common interests of the international community as a whole.”**  
- They are linked to **fundamental values** that cannot be derogated from.  
- They appear in:  
  - Article 48(1) of the Articles on State Responsibility  
  - Articles 49(1)–(2) of the Responsibility of International Organizations  
  [United Nations - Office of Legal Affairs](https://legal.un.org/ilc/reports/2025/english/annex3.pdf)

The ILC confirms:

- Erga omnes obligations are **customary international law**,  
- recognized in multiple ICJ judgments since *Barcelona Traction*.  
  [United Nations - Office of Legal Affairs](https://legal.un.org/ilc/reports/2025/english/annex3.pdf)

## **6. Standing and Actio Popularis**

The Max Planck Encyclopedia (2024) explicitly links erga omnes obligations to:

- **actio popularis**,  
- **universal standing**,  
- **community‑interest litigation**,  
- **countermeasures by non‑injured states**.  
  [Oxford Public International Law](https://opil.ouplaw.com/display/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400?d=/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400&p=emailAyklF7.cHLMDM)

This means:

- Any state may invoke responsibility for breaches of erga omnes obligations.  
- The traditional “injured state” requirement does not apply.  
- The doctrine opens the door to **public‑interest enforcement**.

This is the doctrinal basis for your argument that individuals or states may act when institutions fail.

---

## **7. Enforcement gaps and systemic failure**

The Cambridge analysis (2025) highlights:

- The ICJ has **not clarified** how erga omnes obligations are enforced.  
- There is **no consistent test** for identifying them.  
- This ambiguity **weakens adjudication** and **undermines enforcement**.  
  [Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/articulation-of-obligations-erga-omnes-and-erga-omnes-partes-by-the-international-court-of-justice-coherence-or-confusion/3DF4307A85200A6A6C7C891FC1FE1C7E)

The ILC (2025) similarly notes:

- Erga omnes obligations are **not explicitly codified** in universal treaties.  
- Enforcement relies on **customary law** and **state practice**, which is uneven.  
  [United Nations - Office of Legal Affairs](https://legal.un.org/ilc/reports/2025/english/annex3.pdf)

This is why erga omnes obligations are often **recognized** but **not acted upon**.

# **SYNTHESIS — Formal Doctrinal Conclusions**

Based on the authoritative sources:

### **1. Erga Omnes obligations are real, binding, and part of customary international law.**  
They are owed to the entire international community.  
  [Oxford Public International Law](https://opil.ouplaw.com/display/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400?d=/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400&p=emailAyklF7.cHLMDM)

### **2. They arise primarily from jus cogens norms.**  
But the two concepts are distinct.  
  [sustainablecatalyst.com](https://sustainablecatalyst.com/jus-cogens-erga-omnes-and-peremptory-norms-in-international-law/)

### **3. The ICJ has created doctrinal confusion.**  
Its articulation is inconsistent and lacks criteria.  
  [Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/articulation-of-obligations-erga-omnes-and-erga-omnes-partes-by-the-international-court-of-justice-coherence-or-confusion/3DF4307A85200A6A6C7C891FC1FE1C7E)

### **4. The ILC confirms their legal consequences.**  
Including invocation by non‑injured states.  
  [United Nations - Office of Legal Affairs](https://legal.un.org/ilc/reports/2025/english/annex3.pdf)

### **5. Erga Omnes obligations support actio popularis.**  
They allow enforcement by any state with a legal interest.  
  [Oxford Public International Law](https://opil.ouplaw.com/display/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400?d=/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1400&p=emailAyklF7.cHLMDM)

### **6. Enforcement remains structurally weak.**  
Because institutions lack clear mechanisms and states often avoid action.  
  [Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/articulation-of-obligations-erga-omnes-and-erga-omnes-partes-by-the-international-court-of-justice-coherence-or-confusion/3DF4307A85200A6A6C7C891FC1FE1C7E)
