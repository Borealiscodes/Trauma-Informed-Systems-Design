“The IHREC Discretionary Form‑Based Human Rights Protection Protocol”

A Satire on Ireland’s NHRI Requiring Paperwork to Recognize Human Rights Violations

`
Artifact: IHREC-Discretionary-Form-Based-Human-Rights-Protection-Protocol-v1.0
Altitude: Structural Parody • Governance Satire • NHRI Performance Analysis
Status: Public-Facing Absurdity

Preamble:
In recognition of Ireland’s proud tradition of procedural minimalism,
and the belief that human rights must never interrupt administrative comfort,
the following protocol governs IHREC’s discretionary approach to protecting rights.

Protocol:

1. All human rights violations must be reported using the prescribed Section 40 form.
   The form must be:
   • requested,
   • awaited,
   • and never actually attached.

2. If the victim of torture or transnational repression cannot complete the form
   due to ongoing danger, collapse of domestic remedies, or systemic obstruction,
   IHREC shall interpret this as:
   “Failure to engage with our discretionary process.”

3. Oral complaints are prohibited.
   IHREC will respond:
   “There is no basis to make an oral complaint.”
   (See Judicial Council, 3 September 2026, for harmonized cross-agency policy.)

4. Written complaints demonstrating collapse of domestic remedies,
   UNCAT violations, CRPD Article 13 breaches, or Paris Principles failures
   shall be acknowledged with:
   “We may, at our discretion, provide legal assistance.”

5. Discretion shall be exercised in accordance with the sacred doctrine:
   “We look forward to hearing from you.”

6. If the victim cites:
   • UNCAT Articles 12 & 13,
   • CRPD Article 13,
   • GANHRI General Observations,
   • ECHR Article 13,
   • EU Charter Article 47,
   IHREC shall respond:
   “Please let us know if you would like the form.”

7. If the victim is in danger, facing refoulement, or experiencing transnational repression,
   IHREC shall remind them:
   “Responsibility rests with you for any legal deadlines.”

8. If the victim demonstrates systemic impunity across:
   • the High Court,
   • the Supreme Court,
   • the Oireachtas,
   • UNCAT,
   • OHCHR Petitions Section,
   IHREC shall maintain its constitutional posture:
   “We may, at our discretion, consider your application.”

9. If the victim requests accessibility accommodations under CRPD Article 13,
   IHREC shall provide:
   “Guidelines on Applications for Assistance under Section 40.”

10. If the victim documents collapse of domestic remedies,
    IHREC shall ensure:
    “We will process your application and contact you if we need further information.”

Conclusion:
This protocol ensures that human rights defenders are protected from the dangers
of accidentally receiving help.
`

- Exposes the contradiction between IHREC’s Paris Principles mandate and its discretionary intake posture.  
- Highlights the systemic comedy of an NHRI responding to transnational repression with “please request the form.”
