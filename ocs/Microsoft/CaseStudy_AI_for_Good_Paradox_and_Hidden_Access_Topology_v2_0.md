# ⭐ **2 — Executive Summary (Updated)**

This case study examines the **AI‑for‑Good Paradox**:

> **Microsoft operates a large humanitarian AI program,  
> yet provides no unified contact pathway — only hidden researcher‑level access discoverable through manual project review.**

This updated insight reveals:

- The AI for Good Lab is **real and reachable**, but only through **non‑obvious, non‑public, non‑centralized researcher nodes**.
- EDAD’s failure was **structurally predictable** because EDAD is not connected to Microsoft Research.
- The absence of a public intake channel is a **governance‑alignment gap**, not a personal failure.
- The hidden‑access topology creates **ethical, continuity, and trauma‑informed risks** for HRDs attempting contact.

The case concludes with a **targeting‑adjusted outreach strategy** and a **comprehensive ethical analysis**.

## **5.1 — Public Narrative Geometry**
AI for Good Lab is framed as:

- humanitarian  
- accessible  
- equity‑driven  
- culturally preservational  
- climate‑responsive  

This creates an expectation of:

- public contact  
- safe intake  
- accessible routing  

## **5.2 — Operational Access Geometry (Updated)**

Actual access requires:

- navigating Microsoft Research  
- manually reviewing each project  
- identifying individual researchers  
- contacting them directly  

This is **not** a public humanitarian access pathway.  
It is a **research‑node topology**.

## **5.3 — Governance Alignment Gap**
Public commitments → “AI for Good”  
Operational reality → “Hidden researcher nodes”

This is a **UNGP Pillar II & III misalignment**.

## **5.4 — Ethical Implication**

- undermines transparency  
- creates inequity  
- increases cognitive load  
- risks retraumatization  
- isolates HRDs  
- contradicts accessibility commitments  
- contradicts equity commitments
