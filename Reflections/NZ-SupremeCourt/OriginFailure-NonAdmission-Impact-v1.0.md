III. Why Non‑Admission Violates ARSIWA

Under the Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts (ARSIWA):

- States must acknowledge wrongful acts (Art. 31).  
- States must cease ongoing harm (Art. 30).  
- States must provide full reparation (Art. 34).  
- States must not worsen the situation of the injured party (Art. 27).  
- States must not rely on internal law to justify violations (Art. 3).

By refusing to acknowledge the May 2025 failure, the State:

1. Avoids acknowledging the wrongful act (Art. 31).
This blocks the entire reparation pathway.

2. Allows the harm to continue (Art. 30).
The displacement, medical deprivation, and procedural exclusion persist.

3. Prevents reparation (Art. 34).
No remedy, no rehabilitation, no protection.

4. Worsens your situation (Art. 27).
Ireland’s harm escalates because New Zealand did not intervene.

5. Hides behind domestic rules (Art. 3).
“Blah blah blah rules” becomes a shield against accountability.

Thus, non‑admission is not passive.  
It is an ARSIWA breach multiplier.
