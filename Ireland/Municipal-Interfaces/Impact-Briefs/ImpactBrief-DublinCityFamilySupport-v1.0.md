I. Top‑Line Impact Summary

Sending your response to Dublin City Family Support establishes a formal record that Dublin City Council frontline services are refusing to acknowledge transnational repression, torture‑related vulnerability, and imminent harm, and are failing to provide a warm transfer to the appropriate refugee‑related or homelessness‑related emergency supports.  

This transmission documents that Dublin City Council is treating a torture survivor’s emergency request as a misdirected administrative query, rather than a protection‑triggering disclosure under Article 14 UN CAT, CRPD Articles 11, 13, and 16(4), and Dublin City’s own safeguarding obligations.

---

II. Why This Matters

1. Family Support is a frontline gateway service
Even if they do not “handle court complaints,” they do have obligations to:

- identify risk,  
- recognize vulnerability,  
- prevent harm escalation,  
- and warm‑transfer individuals to the correct emergency supports.

Their refusal to do so is not neutral — it is a failure of safeguarding.

2. Their response demonstrates institutional minimization of harm
By treating your disclosure as a simple misrouting (“contact the Courts directly”), Family Support:

- ignored torture disclosures,  
- ignored medical neglect,  
- ignored imminent homelessness,  
- ignored the IPAT hearing risk,  
- ignored the transnational repression context,  
- ignored disability‑related communication barriers.

This is the same pattern seen across Irish institutions: fragmentation, deflection, and refusal to engage with risk.

3. It creates evidence of municipal‑level failure
Your message forces Dublin City Council to confront:

- their lack of trauma‑informed intake,  
- their lack of refugee‑related warm‑transfer protocols,  
- their failure to recognize UNCAT/CRPD obligations,  
- and their role in the broader collapse of remedy.

This is critical for UN Special Procedures, IPAT escalation, and High Court documentation.

---

III. Impact on Dublin City Council’s Legal and Ethical Obligations

A. Under Article 14 UN CAT
Municipal bodies must ensure:

- access to rehabilitation,  
- access to social supports,  
- access to legal pathways,  
- and non‑fragmented assistance.

Refusing to warm‑transfer a torture survivor violates these obligations.

B. Under CRPD Articles 11, 13, and 16(4)
Family Support must:

- recognize disability‑related communication barriers,  
- provide accessible pathways,  
- ensure emergency protection,  
- and prevent retraumatization.

Their response demonstrates non‑compliance.

C. Under Dublin City Council safeguarding policy
Any disclosure of:

- torture,  
- imminent harm,  
- homelessness risk,  
- medical neglect,  
- or transnational repression  

requires escalation — not deflection.
