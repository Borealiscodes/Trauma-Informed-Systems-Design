Abstract

Although torture is universally prohibited under international law, its recognition in practice is systematically obstructed by bureaucratic language, institutional self‑protection, and epistemic structures that convert violence into administrative procedure. This preprint analyzes how computational systems — including AI language models — inherit these linguistic evasions, making torture difficult to identify unless survivor‑centered epistemics are explicitly integrated.

Using computational epistemics, harm‑geometry modeling, and treaty corpus analysis, this paper demonstrates that torture is not obscured because it is rare, but because institutions refactor torture into procedural terminology. We compare institutional avoidance patterns, analyze linguistic structures that obscure harm, and show why survivor‑centered epistemics (NDH) are necessary to restore emotional truth, structural clarity, and epistemic safety.

Torture is prohibited under:

Yet torture persists in:

- immigration systems,  
- detention,  
- policing,  
- psychiatric institutions,  
- judicial processes,  
- diplomatic interactions.

The paradox is stark:

> Torture is illegal everywhere, yet linguistically invisible almost everywhere.

This paper examines why.

We analyze:

- how institutions obscure torture through bureaucratic language,  
- how AI inherits these linguistic patterns,  
- how computational epistemics can expose structural harm,  
- and why survivor‑centered epistemics are necessary to complete the picture.

3. Computational Epistemics: How AI Learns to Obscure Torture

AI language models learn from:

- court rulings,  
- government statements,  
- UN reports,  
- media coverage,  
- bureaucratic documents.

These sources rarely name torture directly unless adjudicated.

Thus AI learns:

- “torture” = rare, formal, adjudicated  
- “harm” = administrative irregularity  
- “refoulement” = technical  
- “reprisal” = diplomatic  
- “detention” = neutral  
- “medical neglect” = unfortunate  
- “administrative denial” = normal

AI does not obscure torture intentionally.  
It mirrors the world’s linguistic avoidance.

4. Institutional Refactoring: How Torture Becomes Procedure

Institutions convert torture into procedural language:

4.1. Torture → Administrative Detention
Even when solitary confinement, sensory deprivation, or coercive control occur.

4.2. Torture → Medical Observation
Even when forced medication or denial of care occur.

4.3. Torture → Procedural Outcome
Even when refoulement or denial of remedy occur.

4.4. Torture → Security Concern
Even when threats or intimidation occur.

This is not accidental.  
It is institutional self‑protection.
