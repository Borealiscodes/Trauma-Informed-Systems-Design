# **Public‑Facing GANHRI Compliance Audit**  
## **Structural Oversight Gaps in the Global Accreditation System for NHRIs**

### **Executive Summary**

The Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions (GANHRI) is responsible for accrediting NHRIs under the **Paris Principles**, ensuring independence, accessibility, pluralism, and effectiveness.  
This audit examines how GANHRI’s oversight mechanisms fail to detect or correct NHRI collapse, resulting in a global protection vacuum.

It is written for policymakers, NGOs, researchers, and oversight bodies seeking to understand why Paris Principles violations persist across jurisdictions.

---

# **1. Purpose of GANHRI Accreditation**

GANHRI accreditation is intended to:

- ensure NHRI independence from government,  
- guarantee accessibility for complainants,  
- uphold pluralistic representation,  
- verify responsiveness and effectiveness,  
- and maintain compliance with international human rights standards.

Accreditation categories (A, B, C) are meant to reflect real‑world performance.

However, accreditation often reflects **formal structure**, not **functional reality**.

---

# **2. Structural Weaknesses in GANHRI Oversight**

GANHRI oversight has several systemic limitations:

### **2.1 Reliance on Self‑Reporting**  
NHRIs submit their own compliance documentation.  
GANHRI does not independently verify claims.

### **2.2 Lack of Investigative Authority**  
GANHRI cannot:

- investigate complaints against NHRIs,  
- review case files,  
- interview affected individuals,  
- or conduct on‑site assessments.

### **2.3 No Emergency Intervention Mechanism**  
GANHRI cannot intervene when:

- an NHRI collapses,  
- a human rights defender is harmed,  
- or a complainant is blacklisted.

### **2.4 No Enforcement Power**  
GANHRI cannot:

- impose corrective measures,  
- suspend accreditation,  
- require reforms,  
- or compel compliance.

### **2.5 No Protection for Complainants**  
Individuals harmed by NHRI misconduct have **no recourse** through GANHRI.

---

# **3. Consequences of Oversight Failure**

GANHRI’s structural limitations allow:

- Paris Principles violations to persist,  
- NHRI collapse to go undetected,  
- human rights defenders to be exposed to harm,  
- and victims of torture or discrimination to lose access to remedy.

This creates a **global accountability vacuum**.

---

# **4. Case Study Integration**

This audit builds on the previous case studies:

- **Vacant Operational Ethical Texture**  
  NHRI ethical collapse is invisible to GANHRI.

- **Judicial Organ Failure**  
  Courts cannot compensate for NHRI collapse, and GANHRI cannot intervene.

Together, these failures create a **total protection void**.

---

# **5. Recommendations for Structural Reform**

### **5.1 Independent Verification**  
GANHRI should conduct external audits rather than relying on self‑reporting.

### **5.2 Investigative Capacity**  
GANHRI should be empowered to:

- review complaints,  
- investigate misconduct,  
- and assess NHRI accessibility.

### **5.3 Emergency Mechanisms**  
GANHRI should establish:

- rapid response teams,  
- emergency review panels,  
- and protective pathways for human rights defenders.

### **5.4 Accreditation Consequences**  
Accreditation should be:

- suspended,  
- downgraded,  
- or conditioned  
when Paris Principles violations occur.

### **5.5 Complainant Protection**  
GANHRI should create a safe reporting pathway for individuals harmed by NHRI misconduct.

---

# **6. Conclusion**

GANHRI accreditation is essential — but insufficient.  
Without independent verification, investigative authority, emergency mechanisms, and enforcement power, GANHRI cannot prevent or correct NHRI collapse.

This audit documents those gaps and provides a foundation for structural reform.
