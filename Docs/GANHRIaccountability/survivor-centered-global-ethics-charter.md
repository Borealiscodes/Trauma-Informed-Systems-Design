# **Survivor‑Centered Global Ethics Charter**  
## **A Universal Ethical Framework for Protecting Human Dignity When Institutions Collapse**

### **Preamble**

When courts, NHRIs, civil society, media, and international bodies fail, survivors lose not only protection and remedy — they lose **ethical recognition**.  
Institutional collapse produces:

- moral injury,  
- ethical abandonment,  
- narrative suppression,  
- delegitimization of victims,  
- and systemic betrayal.

This Charter establishes a **survivor‑centered global ethical foundation** for:

- truth‑seeking,  
- accountability,  
- restorative justice,  
- memory preservation,  
- solidarity,  
- and non‑recurrence.

It is grounded in Montgomery’s *Concerted Collusion* (2022), your documented evidence of multiagency suppression, and global human rights norms.

---

# **1. Foundational Ethical Principles**

These ten principles form the ethical core of survivor‑centered justice.

### **1.1 Dignity Is Non‑Negotiable**
Every person has inherent dignity that cannot be suspended, denied, or ignored.  
**Dignity principle**

### **1.2 Survivors Define Truth**
Survivors determine what happened, how it is described, and what is shared.  
**Survivor truth**

### **1.3 Harm Must Be Acknowledged**
No healing or justice is possible without acknowledgment.  
**Acknowledgment ethics**

### **1.4 Trauma‑Informed Practice Is Mandatory**
Safety, choice, collaboration, trust, empowerment.  
**Trauma-informed ethics**

### **1.5 Accessibility Is an Ethical Obligation**
Accessibility is not a courtesy — it is a moral requirement.  
**Accessibility ethics**

### **1.6 Transparency Is a Moral Duty**
Secrecy enables harm; transparency prevents it.  
**Transparency ethics**

### **1.7 Solidarity Is a Collective Responsibility**
Communities must protect survivors and HRDs.  
**Solidarity ethics**

### **1.8 Memory Is Sacred**
Memory must be preserved through museums, archives, art, testimony.  
**Memory ethics**

### **1.9 Accountability Is Non‑Optional**
Institutions must be held accountable for harm and suppression.  
**Accountability ethics**

### **1.10 Non‑Recurrence Is a Moral Imperative**
Prevention is not policy — it is ethics.  
**Non-recurrence ethics**

# **2. Ethical Duties for Institutions**

Institutions across sectors have affirmative ethical duties.

### **2.1 Courts**
- accept filings,  
- protect survivors,  
- avoid gatekeeping,  
- uphold dignity.  
**Court ethics**

### **2.2 NHRIs**
- respond promptly,  
- investigate impartially,  
- avoid retaliation.  
**NHRI ethics**

### **2.3 Civil Society**
- support HRDs,  
- avoid complicity,  
- act in good faith.  
**Civil society ethics**

### **2.4 Media**
- report truthfully,  
- avoid suppression,  
- protect vulnerable sources.  
**Media ethics**

### **2.5 International Bodies**
- intervene when domestic institutions fail,  
- uphold international norms.  
**International ethics**

---

# **3. Ethical Duties for Communities**

Communities must:

- support survivors,  
- challenge harmful narratives,  
- preserve memory,  
- build solidarity networks,  
- participate in restorative processes.

**Community ethics**

---

# **4. Ethical Rights of Survivors**

Survivors have **rights**, not obligations.

### **4.1 Survivors Have No Ethical Duties**
Survivors are not responsible for educating institutions or participating in justice processes.

### **4.2 Survivors Have the Right to Safety**
Physical, psychological, digital, and social safety.

### **4.3 Survivors Have the Right to Recognition**
Their experiences must be acknowledged without skepticism or minimization.

### **4.4 Survivors Have the Right to Narrative Control**
They decide what is shared, when, and how.

**Survivor rights**

---

# **5. The Right to Heal Through Agency (Updated Section)**

## **5.1 Healing Is a Right, Not a Duty**
Survivors may withdraw, rest, decline participation, or engage only when ready.  
Healing is not a performance or expectation — it is a **human right**.

## **5.2 Agency Is Central to Healing**
Survivors define:

- what healing looks like,  
- what pace feels safe,  
- what support is acceptable,  
- what remains private.

**Survivor agency**

## **5.3 Healing Pathways Are Diverse**
Creative expression, silence, community support, therapy, activism, ritual, storytelling, disengagement — all valid.

**Healing pathways**

## **5.4 Survivors Control Their Narrative**
Narrative control is both healing and resistance.

**Narrative control**

## **5.5 Survivors May Heal Outside Institutions**
Avoiding harmful institutions is self‑protection, not avoidance.

**Healing outside institutions**

## **5.6 Community Support Must Respect Agency**
Support must be offered, never imposed.

**Community support ethics**
