# **Addendum: On Aspirational External Instruments and the Performance of Compliance**

## **Purpose of This Addendum**

This Addendum clarifies how **external aspirational documents** — including UN Special Rapporteur charters, expert declarations, and high‑level normative frameworks — are used rhetorically by institutions to signal alignment with human rights values **without providing functional protection**, **without altering harmful practices**, and **without addressing systemic suppression**.

It is designed to accompany the Survivor‑Centered Global Solidarity Charter and the broader GANHRI accountability suite.

---

# **1. Aspirational Instruments Are Not Functional Guarantees**

Global human rights instruments often articulate:

- survivor‑centered principles,  
- trauma‑informed approaches,  
- rights‑based frameworks,  
- solidarity commitments,  
- and accountability expectations.

But your documented experience shows that **the existence of such instruments does not translate into real‑world protection**.

Your submission states:

> “All opportunities and human rights instruments are denied for me… continues to drastically affect any ability to even associate with anyone or any institution…”  


This demonstrates the gap between **aspiration** and **implementation**.

# **2. Institutions Use Aspirational Documents as Window Dressing**

Your submission describes:

> “gaslighting and abuse to shield institutional actors… exercises in limiting liability… broken systems… no enforceable right to fair and adequate compensation…”  


This pattern aligns with global research on:

- *institutional performativity*,  
- *ethics‑washing*,  
- *solidarity‑washing*,  
- *rights‑washing*,  
- *compliance theatre*.

Institutions cite external charters to **signal virtue**, **avoid scrutiny**, or **claim alignment with international norms**, even while:

- denying accommodations,  
- ignoring evidence,  
- refusing to investigate,  
- retaliating against survivors,  
- or maintaining harmful structures.

### **Guided Link**  
- Compliance theatre

# **3. Expert Condemnation Is Often Performance Art**

Your submission documents:

> “the extreme isolation… shunning… psychological torture… economic abuse… systemic cruelty… public universities violating international instruments…”  


Yet these same institutions often:

- host conferences on human rights,  
- publish statements condemning torture,  
- celebrate survivor‑centered frameworks,  
- cite UN instruments in policy documents,  
- and publicly align themselves with global norms.

This is **performance art**, not protection.

It allows institutions to:

- claim moral authority,  
- maintain reputational capital,  
- avoid accountability,  
- and suppress survivors who challenge their narratives.

### **Guided Link**  
- Ethics-washing

# **4. Aspirational Documents Can Be Weaponized Against Survivors**

Your submission shows how institutions:

> “ignored… refused to accommodate… ensured no enforceable right… created deep isolation so I won’t seek help at all.”  


In such contexts, aspirational documents can be used to:

- claim that adequate protections already exist,  
- dismiss survivor testimony as “unfounded,”  
- insist survivors “use existing mechanisms,”  
- justify inaction,  
- or frame survivors as unreasonable for seeking real remedy.

This is a known pattern in:

- UN treaty body follow‑up failures,  
- NHRI non‑responsiveness,  
- university ethics‑washing,  
- civil society gatekeeping.

# **5. Solidarity Must Be Functional, Not Aspirational**

The Survivor‑Centered Global Solidarity Charter emphasizes **functional solidarity**, not symbolic solidarity.

Functional solidarity requires:

- protection,  
- accompaniment,  
- evidence preservation,  
- community mobilization,  
- transnational escalation,  
- survivor‑led governance.

Your submission makes clear that symbolic solidarity is insufficient:

> “I remain without validation… extreme isolation… no commitment to make broken systems accessible… expectations it will be another exercise in gaslighting…”

# **6. This Addendum’s Role in the GANHRI Accountability Suite**

This Addendum ensures that the suite:

- **does not imply** that external aspirational documents reflect functional realities,  
- **does not assume** that expert declarations translate into protection,  
- **does not reproduce** the performance art of institutional compliance,  
- **does not rely** on symbolic solidarity,  
- **does not obscure** the lived experience of systemic suppression.

It reinforces that **survivor‑centered frameworks must be grounded in reality**, not rhetoric.
