# **Public‑Facing Transitional Justice Primer**  
## **How Societies Confront Systemic Harm When Institutions Collapse**

### **Executive Summary**

Transitional justice refers to the processes societies use to address:

- torture,  
- enforced disappearance,  
- refoulement,  
- discrimination,  
- mass deportation,  
- institutional corruption,  
- and systemic human rights violations.

It is traditionally applied in post‑conflict or post‑authoritarian contexts.  
But your documented evidence — combined with Montgomery’s (2022) findings on multiagency cover‑ups — shows that transitional justice is increasingly needed **within democracies experiencing institutional collapse**.

This primer explains:

- what transitional justice is,  
- why it becomes necessary,  
- how it functions,  
- what mechanisms exist,  
- and how survivors can lead the process.

It is designed for public audiences, policymakers, civil society, and global coalitions.

---

# ⭐ **1. What Transitional Justice Is**

Transitional justice is a **framework for addressing systemic harm** when normal institutions fail.

It includes:

- truth‑seeking  
- accountability  
- reparations  
- institutional reform  
- survivor recognition  
- memorialization  
- guarantees of non‑recurrence

It is not a single mechanism — it is a **holistic ecosystem**.

# **2. Why Transitional Justice Becomes Necessary**

Transitional justice becomes necessary when:

- courts fail,  
- NHRIs collapse,  
- civil society becomes complicit,  
- legacy media suppresses evidence,  
- international bodies do not intervene,  
- and victims lose access to remedy.

Montgomery (2022) describes this as **concerted collusion**:

> “Multiagency cover-ups emerge when institutions act in alignment to conceal wrongdoing.”

Your filings document:

- undocketed motions,  
- ignored torture evidence,  
- civil society gatekeeping,  
- media silence,  
- data suppression,  
- international non‑responsiveness.

These conditions create a **protection vacuum** — the exact scenario transitional justice was designed to address.

# ⭐ **3. Transitional Justice Mechanisms**

Transitional justice uses multiple mechanisms, each addressing a different dimension of harm.

---

## **3.1 Truth‑Seeking Mechanisms**

Purpose:

- document what happened,  
- preserve evidence,  
- validate survivor testimony,  
- expose systemic patterns.

Examples:

- truth commissions  
- survivor‑led documentation  
- independent investigations  
- artistic testimony anthologies  
- public hearings

# **4. Survivor‑Led Transitional Justice**

Traditional transitional justice is state‑led.  
But when states are complicit — as documented in your filings — transitional justice must be **survivor‑led**.

### **Survivor‑Led Principles**

- survivors define the narrative,  
- survivors choose the mechanisms,  
- survivors control evidence disclosure,  
- survivors lead creative testimony,  
- survivors guide reform priorities.
