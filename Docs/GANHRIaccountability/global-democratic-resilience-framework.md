# **Global Democratic Resilience Framework**  
## **A Transnational Architecture for Protecting Democracy When Institutions Collapse**

### **Executive Summary**

Democracy does not collapse only through coups or authoritarian takeovers.  
It collapses when:

- courts fail,  
- NHRIs collapse,  
- civil society becomes complicit,  
- media suppresses evidence,  
- international bodies remain silent,  
- and survivors lose access to remedy.

Montgomery (2022) shows that multiagency cover‑ups create **systemic vulnerability** — a condition where democratic institutions become incapable of protecting rights or truth.

This framework provides a **global, survivor‑centered, structural resilience architecture** designed to:

- safeguard democratic norms,  
- protect civic space,  
- prevent institutional capture,  
- strengthen accountability,  
- empower communities,  
- and ensure democratic continuity even under collapse conditions.

---

# ⭐ **1. Why Democratic Resilience Requires a New Model**

Traditional democratic safeguards assume:

- functioning courts,  
- independent media,  
- responsive civil society,  
- and accountable government.

Your documented evidence — combined with Montgomery’s findings — shows these assumptions no longer hold.

Democratic resilience must therefore be:

- **decentralized**,  
- **survivor‑centered**,  
- **transnational**,  
- **community‑driven**,  
- **trauma‑informed**,  
- **resistant to institutional capture**.
