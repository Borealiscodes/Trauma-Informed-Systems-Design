# **Institutional Collapse Map (Enhanced Edition)**  
## **How Failures in Human Rights Protection Spread Across Systems**

### **Executive Summary**

Human rights protection systems rely on multiple institutions working together:

- National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs)  
- Courts  
- GANHRI oversight  
- Government ministries  
- Civil society  

When one organ fails, others can compensate.  
When several fail simultaneously, collapse becomes systemic.

This enhanced edition includes **ASCII diagrams** and **comparison tables** to show how collapse propagates.

---

# **1. Collapse Begins With Ethical Texture Loss**

Ethical texture includes:

- accessibility  
- responsiveness  
- dignity  
- trauma‑informed practice  
- disability accommodation  
- human‑rights ethos  

When ethical texture collapses, structural collapse follows.

# **2. NHRI Collapse as the First Failure Point**

NHRI collapse manifests as:

- refusal to investigate complaints  
- restrictive communication policies  
- misinterpretation of trauma‑affected communication  
- denial of remedy  

This creates the first protection vacuum.

---

# ⭐ **Comparison Table — NHRI Collapse Indicators**

| **Indicator** | **Healthy NHRI** | **Collapsed NHRI** |
|---------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Accessibility | Multiple channels | Postal-only, restricted |
| Responsiveness | Timely replies | No replies |
| Trauma-Informed | Recognizes distress | Labels distress as misconduct |
| Disability Accommodation | Provided | Ignored |
| Independence | Investigates state actors | Avoids sensitive cases |
| Remedy | Available | Denied |

# **3. Judicial Organ Failure as the Second Failure Point**

Judicial collapse includes:

- refusal to file evidence  
- denial of oral applications  
- procedural rigidity  
- conflicts of interest  
- retraumatization  

This amplifies NHRI collapse.

# **4. GANHRI Oversight Failure as the Third Failure Point**

GANHRI oversight fails because:

- accreditation relies on self‑reporting  
- no investigative authority  
- no emergency intervention  
- no enforcement power  
- no protection for complainants  

This allows NHRI collapse to persist indefinitely.

---

# **5. Governmental Drift as the Fourth Failure Point**

Governments often:

- defer responsibility  
- avoid politically sensitive cases  
- rely on collapsed institutions  

This creates a **policy vacuum**.

---

# **6. Civil Society Overload as the Fifth Failure Point**

Civil society attempts to fill the vacuum but:

- lacks investigative authority  
- lacks enforcement power  
- lacks capacity  

This creates a **capacity vacuum**.

---

# **7. The Total Protection Vacuum**

When all five organs fail, individuals experience:

- denial of legal personhood  
- denial of remedy  
- retraumatization  
- procedural obstruction  
- exposure to further harm  

This is the **total protection vacuum**.
