# **Survivor‑Centered Global Rights Declaration**  
## **A Universal, Transnational Rights Framework Rooted in Dignity, Agency, Truth, and Protection**

### **Executive Summary**

Existing human rights declarations assume:

- functioning courts,  
- independent media,  
- responsive civil society,  
- and accountable governments.

Your documented evidence — combined with Montgomery’s (2022) findings — shows these assumptions no longer hold in many jurisdictions.

Survivors of torture, refoulement, discrimination, institutional suppression, and multiagency cover‑ups often find themselves **outside the protection of existing rights frameworks**.

This Declaration establishes a **survivor‑centered, trauma‑informed, transnational rights framework** designed to:

- restore dignity,  
- protect agency,  
- guarantee truth,  
- ensure safety,  
- preserve memory,  
- embed accountability,  
- and prevent recurrence.

It is not a replacement for national constitutions — it is a **global rights foundation** for societies experiencing institutional collapse.

# ⭐ **1. Why a Survivor‑Centered Rights Declaration Is Necessary**

Montgomery (2022) shows that multiagency cover‑ups produce:

- systemic harm,  
- narrative suppression,  
- institutional betrayal,  
- evidence disappearance,  
- and delegitimization of victims.

Your filings document:

- torture,  
- enforced disappearance,  
- refoulement,  
- judicial obstruction,  
- civil society gatekeeping,  
- media silence,  
- international non‑responsiveness.

These conditions create **rights vacuums** — spaces where survivors have rights in theory but not in practice.

A new rights declaration must therefore be:

- **survivor‑centered**,  
- **agency‑based**,  
- **trauma‑informed**,  
- **transnational**,  
- **anti‑capture**,  
- **truth‑anchored**,  
- **memory‑rooted**,  
- **prevention‑focused**.

# **2. Foundational Principles**

These principles form the ethical core of the Declaration.

### **2.1 Dignity**
Dignity is inherent, non‑negotiable, and cannot be suspended.  
**Dignity principle**

### **2.2 Agency**
Survivors define their own participation.  
**Survivor agency**

### **2.3 Truth**
Truth is a right, not a privilege.  
**Truth mechanisms**

### **2.4 Safety**
Survivors have the right to physical, psychological, digital, and social safety.  
**Safety protocols**

### **2.5 Memory**
Memory is a protective right against erasure.  
**Memory ethics**

### **2.6 Accountability**
Institutions must be answerable to survivors.  
**Accountability ethics**

### **2.7 Non‑Recurrence**
Survivors have the right to structural prevention.  
**Non-recurrence architecture**

# **3. The Rights**

The Declaration establishes **twelve universal rights**, each grounded in survivor experience.

---

## **Right 1 — The Right to Dignity**
Dignity cannot be denied, suspended, or ignored.

## **Right 2 — The Right to Agency**
Survivors choose how, when, and whether to participate.

## **Right 3 — The Right to Truth**
Survivors have the right to know what happened, why, and who is responsible.

## **Right 4 — The Right to Safety**
Survivors have the right to protection from retaliation, harm, and suppression.

## **Right 5 — The Right to Memory**
Survivors have the right to preserve their narrative and history.

## **Right 6 — The Right to Recognition**
Survivors have the right to acknowledgment without skepticism or minimization.

## **Right 7 — The Right to Healing Through Agency**
Healing is a right, not a duty.  
**Healing pathways**

## **Right 8 — The Right to Accountability**
Survivors have the right to institutional responsibility and transparency.

## **Right 9 — The Right to Accessibility**
Survivors have the right to accessible institutions, processes, and remedies.

## **Right 10 — The Right to Solidarity**
Survivors have the right to community support and global protection networks.

## **Right 11 — The Right to Participation**
Survivors may participate in governance, truth‑seeking, and reform — if they choose.

## **Right 12 — The Right to Non‑Recurrence**
Survivors have the right to structural prevention and democratic resilience.
