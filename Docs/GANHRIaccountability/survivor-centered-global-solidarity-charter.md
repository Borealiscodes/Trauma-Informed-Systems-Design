# **Survivor‑Centered Global Solidarity Charter**  
## **A Transnational Framework for Collective Protection, Shared Power, and Survivor‑Led Global Action**

### **Executive Summary**

Global solidarity is not charity, sympathy, or symbolic support.  
It is **collective protection**, **shared responsibility**, and **transnational action** grounded in survivor leadership.

The UN Special Rapporteur’s 2026 Survivors’ Charter emphasizes that survivors are *rights‑holders, advocates, and architects of positive change* — not passive witnesses.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/03/new-survivors-charter-sets-out-global-blueprint-justice-after-torture)  
This Global Solidarity Charter builds on that foundation, expanding solidarity beyond torture survivors to include:

- survivors of institutional suppression,  
- HRDs,  
- whistleblowers,  
- displaced persons,  
- journalists,  
- artists,  
- community organizers,  
- and civic actors facing retaliation.

It establishes a **global solidarity ethic**, a **solidarity infrastructure**, and **solidarity obligations** for communities, institutions, and international bodies.

---

# ⭐ **1. Why a Global Solidarity Charter Is Necessary**

Search results show that survivors worldwide describe:

- disbelief,  
- exclusion,  
- bureaucratic indifference,  
- retaliation,  
- and long struggles for recognition.   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/stories/2026/03/torture-survivors-push-landmark-charter-turn-rights-reality)

The UN Special Rapporteur notes that survivors “are not merely witnesses to atrocity — they are rights‑holders, advocates, and architects of positive change.”   [OHCHR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/03/new-survivors-charter-sets-out-global-blueprint-justice-after-torture)

Yet global solidarity systems remain fragmented, inconsistent, and often symbolic rather than protective.

A Global Solidarity Charter is needed to:

- unify global survivor‑centered principles,  
- establish shared obligations,  
- create transnational protection networks,  
- amplify survivor leadership,  
- and ensure solidarity is actionable, not rhetorical.

# **2. Foundational Solidarity Principles**

These principles form the ethical core of global solidarity.

### **2.1 Solidarity Begins With Dignity**
Solidarity is impossible without recognition of inherent dignity.  
**Dignity**

### **2.2 Solidarity Centers Survivors**
Survivors define solidarity needs, priorities, and boundaries.  
**Survivor agency**

### **2.3 Solidarity Protects**
Solidarity must reduce risk, not increase it.  
**Protection protocols**

### **2.4 Solidarity Amplifies Truth**
Solidarity requires amplifying suppressed narratives.  
**Truth mechanisms**

### **2.5 Solidarity Preserves Memory**
Memory is a collective responsibility.  
**Memory ethics**

### **2.6 Solidarity Is Transnational**
Harm crosses borders; solidarity must too.  
**Solidarity networks**

### **2.7 Solidarity Prevents Recurrence**
Solidarity is a prevention mechanism.  
**Non-recurrence**

# **3. Solidarity Rights**

Survivors have **solidarity rights** that complement the global rights declaration.

### **3.1 Right to Global Recognition**
Survivors must be believed and acknowledged across borders.

### **3.2 Right to Global Protection**
Survivors have the right to transnational safety networks.

### **3.3 Right to Global Agency**
Survivors choose how solidarity is expressed.

### **3.4 Right to Global Memory**
Survivor narratives must be preserved globally.

### **3.5 Right to Global Participation**
Survivors may participate in global decision‑making — if they choose.

### **3.6 Right to Global Support**
Communities worldwide share responsibility for survivor wellbeing.

---

# **4. Solidarity Duties**

Solidarity requires **obligations**, not just values.

---

## **4.1 Duties of Communities**
Communities must:

- protect survivors,  
- challenge harmful narratives,  
- preserve memory,  
- build solidarity networks.

**Community ethics**

---

## **4.2 Duties of Institutions**
Institutions must:

- avoid complicity,  
- amplify survivor voices,  
- provide safe channels,  
- prevent retaliation.

**Institutional ethics**

---

## **4.3 Duties of Global Actors**
International bodies must:

- intervene when domestic systems fail,  
- uphold survivor‑centered norms,  
- support global protection networks.

**International ethics**

## **Pillar 4 — Solidarity Actions**
Rapid‑response mobilization, advocacy, and public awareness.

Survivor‑led councils guiding global solidarity policy.
