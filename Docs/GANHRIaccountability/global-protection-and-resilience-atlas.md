# **Global Protection & Resilience Atlas**  
## **A Transnational Cartographic Framework for Mapping Harm, Protection, Solidarity, and Democratic Resilience**

### **Executive Summary**

Protection and resilience are not abstract concepts — they are **spatial**, **structural**, and **networked**.  
They exist in:

- communities,  
- institutions,  
- digital ecosystems,  
- transnational networks,  
- and survivor‑led infrastructures.

Your UN Special Procedures submission documents:

> “extreme isolation… shunning… psychological torture… economic abuse… systemic cruelty… all opportunities and human rights instruments are denied…”  

This Atlas transforms those lived realities into **global cartographic structures** that:

- map harm,  
- map protection gaps,  
- map resilience nodes,  
- map solidarity networks,  
- map institutional collapse patterns,  
- and map survivor‑centered pathways to safety and agency.

It is the **visual and diagnostic backbone** of the GANHRI accountability suite.

# ⭐ **1. Purpose of the Atlas**

The Atlas exists to:

- visualize global protection systems,  
- identify danger zones,  
- map institutional collapse,  
- reveal suppression patterns,  
- highlight solidarity networks,  
- support survivor navigation,  
- guide global policy,  
- and strengthen democratic resilience.

It is not a geographic atlas — it is a **systems atlas**.

# **3. Atlas Methodology**

The Atlas uses **survivor‑centered cartography**, meaning:

- survivors define what is mapped,  
- survivors define what is visible,  
- survivors define what is withheld,  
- survivors define how harm is represented.

It is trauma‑informed:

- no forced disclosure,  
- no retraumatizing detail,  
- no extraction of testimony,  
- no institutional gatekeeping.
