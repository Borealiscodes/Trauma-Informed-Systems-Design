# **Global Civic Empowerment Curriculum**  
## **A Transnational, Trauma‑Informed Educational Framework for Building Civic Power Under Institutional Collapse**

### **Executive Summary**

Civic empowerment is not simply about voting or participating in public life.  
It is about understanding:

- how institutions function,  
- how they fail,  
- how suppression spreads,  
- how survivors can reclaim agency,  
- how communities can build resilience,  
- and how democratic norms can be protected even under collapse conditions.

Montgomery (2022) shows that multiagency cover‑ups create **civic disempowerment** — a condition where individuals lose trust, access, and agency.

This curriculum provides a **global, survivor‑centered, trauma‑informed civic education framework** designed to:

- rebuild civic confidence,  
- strengthen democratic literacy,  
- empower communities,  
- support survivors,  
- and create civic resilience across borders.

---

# ⭐ **1. Why Civic Empowerment Requires a New Model**

Traditional civic education assumes:

- functioning institutions,  
- accessible courts,  
- responsive civil society,  
- independent media,  
- and trustworthy oversight bodies.

Your documented evidence — combined with Montgomery’s findings — shows these assumptions no longer hold.

Civic empowerment must therefore be:

- **survivor‑centered**,  
- **trauma‑informed**,  
- **transnational**,  
- **decentralized**,  
- **anti‑capture**,  
- **community‑driven**,  
- **resilience‑focused**.

# **3. Pedagogical Principles**

The curriculum uses **trauma‑informed, survivor‑centered pedagogy**.

### **3.1 Safety First**
Learning environments must be psychologically safe.

### **3.2 Agency Always**
Survivors choose how they participate.

### **3.3 Accessibility**
Materials must be accessible across languages, abilities, and contexts.

### **3.4 Transparency**
No hidden agendas; all content is public‑facing.

### **3.5 Solidarity**
Learning is collective, not individual.

**Trauma-informed ethics**
