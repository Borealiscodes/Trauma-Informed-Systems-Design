# **Public‑Facing Parliamentary Accountability Recommendations**  
## **Legislative Actions to Address NHRI Collapse, Judicial Failure, and GANHRI Oversight Gaps**

### **Executive Summary**

Human rights protection systems across multiple jurisdictions are experiencing systemic collapse.  
Parliament has the authority — and responsibility — to intervene when:

- National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs) fail,  
- courts become inaccessible,  
- oversight bodies cannot enforce compliance,  
- and individuals lose access to remedy, justice, and recognition.

This chapter provides **clear, actionable recommendations** for Parliament to restore ethical texture, strengthen oversight, and rebuild public trust.

It is based on findings from:

- the **NHRI collapse case study**  
- the **judicial organ failure case study**  
- the **GANHRI compliance audit**  
- the **trauma‑informed NHRI reform blueprint**  
- the **institutional collapse map**  
- and the **synthesis chapter**

---

# **1. Legislative Priority: Restore Ethical Texture in Human Rights Institutions**

Ethical texture — accessibility, responsiveness, dignity, trauma‑informed practice, disability accommodation — is the foundation of protection.

Parliament should legislate:

### **1.1 Accessibility Standards for NHRIs**
- Mandatory acceptance of email, phone, remote submissions, and oral applications.  
- Prohibition of restrictive communication policies (e.g., postal‑only).  
- Mandatory disability accommodations under CRPD Article 13.

### **1.2 Trauma‑Informed Practice Requirements**
- Mandatory training for all NHRI staff.  
- Recognition of trauma‑affected communication as protected expression.  
- Prohibition of punitive responses to distress.

### **1.3 Dignity and Non‑Retaliation Guarantees**
- Legal prohibition on blacklisting complainants.  
- Mandatory review of any communication restrictions.  
- External oversight of NHRI complaint handling.

# **2. Legislative Priority: Judicial Accessibility and Anti‑Gatekeeping Measures**

Courts must be accessible to all individuals, including those experiencing trauma, disability, or crisis.

Parliament should legislate:

### **2.1 Registry Accessibility Requirements**
- Mandatory acceptance of filings in multiple formats.  
- Prohibition of arbitrary refusal to file evidence.  
- Mandatory disability accommodations.

### **2.2 Anti‑Gatekeeping Rules**
- Prohibition of procedural barriers not found in law.  
- Mandatory review of any refusal to accept filings.  
- Independent oversight of registry conduct.

### **2.3 Nemo Judex Safeguards**
- Courts cannot adjudicate cases involving their own conduct.  
- Mandatory transfer to an independent venue.

# **3. Legislative Priority: Oversight Reform for NHRIs and GANHRI Alignment**

GANHRI oversight is insufficient.  
Parliament can strengthen domestic oversight to compensate.

### **3.1 Independent NHRI Oversight Body**
- External audits  
- Complaint review  
- Accessibility monitoring  
- Public reporting

### **3.2 Emergency Intervention Mechanisms**
- Rapid review panels  
- Protective pathways for human rights defenders  
- Emergency filing channels

### **3.3 Accreditation Consequences**
- Domestic accreditation can be suspended or conditioned.  
- GANHRI accreditation should not be treated as conclusive.

# **4. Legislative Priority: Protect Human Rights Defenders (HRDs)**

Parliament should legislate:

### **4.1 HRD Protection Unit**
- Monitors retaliation risks  
- Provides safe communication channels  
- Ensures HRDs are not silenced

### **4.2 Anti‑Retaliation Statutes**
- Criminal penalties for retaliatory conduct  
- Civil remedies for victims  
- Mandatory reporting of retaliation incidents

### **4.3 Emergency HRD Pathways**
- Rapid review  
- Protective orders  
- Safe evidence submission channels

---

# **5. Legislative Priority: Public Transparency and Reporting**

Parliament should require:

- annual NHRI performance reports,  
- accessibility audits,  
- complaint‑handling statistics,  
- disability accommodation compliance reports,  
- and public publication of oversight findings.

Transparency restores trust.

---

# **6. Conclusion**

Parliament has the authority to rebuild human rights protection systems.  
These recommendations provide a clear, actionable path to:

- restore ethical texture,  
- strengthen oversight,  
- protect human rights defenders,  
- and ensure compliance with the Paris Principles.

This chapter completes the legislative portion of the GANHRI accountability suite.
