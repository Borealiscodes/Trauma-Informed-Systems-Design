# **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

Erga Omnes obligations are duties owed **to the international community as a whole**, not to any single state. They arise from violations of **fundamental norms** such as:

- genocide  
- torture  
- apartheid  
- slavery  
- aggression  
- violations of self‑determination  

This case study examines:

1. **The doctrinal structure of Erga Omnes obligations**  
2. **Their relationship to jus cogens norms**  
3. **ICJ inconsistencies and enforcement gaps**  
4. **ILC clarifications (2024–2026)**  
5. **Standing and actio popularis implications**  
6. **Trauma‑systems consequences when institutions fail to act**

ASCII diagrams, math expressions, and comparison tables are integrated directly into the analysis to show how Erga Omnes obligations function — and fail — within real systems.

# **META ANALYSIS (Systems‑Level)**

## **1. What Erga Omnes Obligations Are**

The ICJ (Barcelona Traction, 1970) defined Erga Omnes obligations as:

> **“obligations owed to the international community as a whole.”**

This means:

- Any state may invoke responsibility.  
- No “injured state” requirement exists.  
- Violations affect **all states**, not just the directly harmed party.

ASCII representation:

```
Harm → Community Interest → Universal Standing
```

---

## **3. Relationship to Jus Cogens**

The ILC (2025) clarifies:

- **Jus Cogens** = hierarchy of norms  
- **Erga Omnes** = structure of obligation  

They overlap but are not identical.

ASCII:

```
Jus Cogens (non-derogable)
        ∩
Erga Omnes (owed to all)
```

---

## **4. ICJ Inconsistencies (2025 Scholarly Consensus)**

Priya Urs (Oxford, 2025) identifies:

- inconsistent criteria  
- unclear definitions  
- blurred distinction between erga omnes and erga omnes partes  
- no enforcement mechanism  
- no standing test  

ASCII:

```
ICJ Output:
   ├─ Recognizes Erga Omnes
   ├─ Does not define criteria
   ├─ Does not clarify standing
   └─ Leaves enforcement ambiguous
```

## **5. ILC Clarifications (2024–2026)**

The ILC confirms:

- Erga Omnes obligations are **customary international law**  
- They appear in Articles 48 and 49 of the State Responsibility Articles  
- They reflect **community interests**  
- They allow **invocation by non‑injured states**

Comparison:

| **Source** | **Position** |
|------------|--------------|
| ICJ | Recognizes but unclear |
| ILC | Recognizes and clarifies |
| Max Planck | Provides criteria |

# **FORMAL CASE BODY**

```
## 1. Definition

Erga Omnes obligations are duties owed to the international community as a whole.
They arise from violations of fundamental norms such as genocide, torture, apartheid,
slavery, aggression, and violations of self-determination.

## 2. Doctrinal Structure

Erga Omnes obligations allow any state to invoke responsibility, regardless of direct
injury. They reflect community interests and are recognized in customary international
law, ICJ jurisprudence, and ILC codification.

## 3. Relationship to Jus Cogens

Most jus cogens norms generate erga omnes obligations, but the concepts are distinct.
Jus cogens norms are non-derogable; erga omnes obligations concern universal standing.

## 4. ICJ Inconsistencies

The ICJ has recognized erga omnes obligations but has not provided clear criteria,
standing rules, or enforcement mechanisms. This ambiguity weakens adjudication.

## 5. ILC Clarifications

The ILC confirms erga omnes obligations in Articles 48 and 49 of the State
Responsibility Articles. They reflect community interests and allow invocation by
non-injured states.

## 6. Standing and Actio Popularis

Erga Omnes obligations support universal standing and actio popularis, enabling
community-interest enforcement when institutions fail to act.

## 7. Systemic Failure

Enforcement gaps arise from ICJ ambiguity, state avoidance, and institutional
limitations. These gaps produce trauma-signal suppression and cruelty normalization
within international systems.
```
