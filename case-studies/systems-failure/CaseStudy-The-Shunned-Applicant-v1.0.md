# **TISD Case Study: “The Shunned Applicant”**  
### *A Trauma-Informed Systems Design Analysis of Multi-Institutional Harm Across Education, Governance, and Human Rights Mechanisms*  
**Period of Harm:** **24 September 2025 – present**

---

## **1. Case Overview (Anonymized)**  
Beginning **24 September 2025**, a neurodivergent human rights defender (“the Applicant”) attempted to access postgraduate education, disability accommodations, and basic institutional protections across multiple public universities and statutory bodies in Ireland and the UK.

Across this period, the Applicant experienced:

- institutional shunning,  
- denial of reasonable accommodations,  
- retaliatory exclusion from campus,  
- threats of police involvement,  
- bureaucratic obstruction,  
- and systemic failure to investigate torture allegations.

The Applicant filed complaints with human rights bodies, regulatory authorities, and oversight institutions. These filings were met with silence, dismissal, or further retaliation.

This case study analyzes the systemic patterns of harm across the entire period **24/9/25–present**.

---

## **2. Core Systemic Failure Modes**  
### **2.1. Shunning as Institutional Harm**  
The Applicant repeatedly reported being ignored or dismissed by institutions.  
From the UN submission:  
> “I am subjected to what would normally be considered indirect bullying in the form of shunning.”  

This is **Passive Harm** — harm emerging from avoidance, silence, and exclusion.

---

### **2.2. Retaliatory Exclusion**  
A university issued an exclusion letter after the Applicant attempted to deliver documentation related to torture complaints.  
From the exclusion letter:  
> “Security have been alerted… should you attend campus… the Gardai will be alerted.”  

This is **Active Harm** — coercive boundary-setting used to suppress reporting.

---

### **2.3. Failure to Provide Reasonable Accommodation**  
The Applicant requested verbal communication accommodations due to disability-related barriers.  
From the UN submission:  
> “I needed a reasonable accommodation… via a verbal application… I was ignored.”  

This is **Structural Harm** — systems not designed for neurodivergent accessibility.

---

### **2.4. Bureaucratic Looping / Administrative Gaslighting**  
Institutions repeatedly required the Applicant to restart processes or re-prove identity.  
From York University correspondence:  
> “The Complaints Team would be unable to handle their complaint as they are not a student.”  

This is **Procedural Harm** — systems that create exhaustion and despair.

---

### **2.5. Suppression of Human Rights Reporting**  
Attempts to report torture were met with threats, exclusion, or dismissal.  
From the complaint:  
> “Threatening me with law enforcement for reporting torture.”  

This is **Reprisal Harm** — retaliation against whistleblowing.

## **4. System Interaction Map (High-Level)**  
### **Nodes**
- Public universities (Ireland, UK)  
- Disability offices  
- Academic registries  
- Human rights bodies (UN Special Procedures, OHCHR)  
- Regulatory authorities (LSRA, SIPO, WRC)  
- Police / security  
- Data protection authorities  
- Immigration / asylum bodies  

### **Edges**
- Complaint → Retaliation  
- Accommodation request → Ignored  
- Torture report → Threat of police  
- Data protection request → Bureaucratic loop  
- Academic application → Blacklisting  
- Human rights filing → No action  

### **Emergent Pattern**
A **multi-node suppression network** formed unintentionally through:

- risk aversion,  
- bureaucratic rigidity,  
- institutional self-protection,  
- epistemic bias against neurodivergent communication.

### Legal Harm  
Failure to investigate torture, retaliation for reporting, denial of procedural fairness.

---

## **6. Systemic Root Causes**  
### Epistemic Bias Against Neurodivergent Communication  
Urgency or atypical communication was misinterpreted as misconduct.

### Institutional Self-Protection Reflex  
Reputational risk was prioritized over human rights obligations.

### Fragmented Accountability Ecosystem  
Each body assumed another was responsible.

### Absence of Trauma-Informed Protocols  
No system recognized the Applicant’s trauma history or adjusted processes accordingly.

---

## **7. TISD Recommendations (Draft)**  
### Trauma-Informed Intake Protocols  
Verbal pathways, high-volume communication accommodations, non-punitive engagement.

### Anti-Reprisal Safeguards  
Prohibit threats of police for reporting harm; independent oversight for HRD complaints.

### Neurodivergent Accessibility Standards  
Communication flexibility; staff training on neurodivergent distress signals.

### Cross-Institutional Harm Monitoring  
Shared redress pathways; multi-body coordination for HRD protection.

### Human Rights Integration  
CAT Article 14 rehabilitation pathways; CRPD Article 13 procedural fairness guarantees.
